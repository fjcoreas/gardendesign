# Influential garden designers and current garden-design creators: signature ideas as "style lenses"

Research notes compiled 14 Nov 2026. Method note: web search worked, but direct page fetches were blocked by the session's network allowlist (en.wikipedia.org, gardensillustrated.com, bethchatto.co.uk, themiddlesizedgarden.co.uk and gardenersdream.co.uk could not be resolved). Most findings below therefore come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Plant palettes and visual hallmarks that could not be confirmed this way are listed under **Inferences** and labelled as general horticultural knowledge, so the report writer can tell them apart from cited fact.

## Q1. Designer profiles: who they are, philosophy, signature moves, plant palette, visual hallmarks, suitable site and client, sources

### Takeaway
The canon falls into three groups. (1) Naturalistic or ecological planters: Oudolf, Pearson, Stuart-Smith, Chatto, Dunnett and Hitchmough, Rainer and West, Filippi, Price. (2) Structure-first designers: Caruncho, Giubbilei, Maynard, Sturgeon, Burle Marx. (3) Romantic or colourist plantspeople: Jekyll, Lloyd, Don, Blom, Frost. For an AI "lens", the clearest renderable signatures are Oudolf's grass-matrix drifts with seedheads, Chatto's and Filippi's gravel with no lawn, Caruncho's geometry and wheat, Maynard's topiary that gives way to meadow, Burle Marx's curving mass-planted bands with mosaic paving, and Lloyd's clashing-colour mixed border. Two key figures died in 2026: Nigel Dunnett (26 April 2026, confirmed) and Fernando Caruncho (reported around August 2026).

### Cited Findings

**Piet Oudolf (Netherlands; New Perennial movement)**
- Philosophy: he puts plant structure and the whole life cycle ahead of flower colour. He has said he never thinks about colour when making planting plans, and he designs plantings to look good in every season, including after flowering — [Search summary of Oudolf sources incl. Hortmag](https://www.hortmag.com/gardens/matrix-planting-garden-design); [Gardenista: 10 ideas to steal from Oudolf](https://www.gardenista.com/posts/10-garden-ideas-to-steal-from-superstar-dutch-designer-piet-oudolf)
- Technique: layered planting. A "matrix" of quiet plants (mostly grasses) is the "dough of a fruitcake". "Primary" structure plants are repeated in clumps through it, and "scatter" plants are added for spontaneity. He uses roughly 70% structure or matrix plants to 30% filler. Matrix grasses should develop early, such as sedges and moor grass (Molinia). Primary and scatter plants are set out first. The method is set out in Oudolf and Kingsbury's book *Planting: A New Perspective* — [Hortmag: Matrix planting approach](https://www.hortmag.com/gardens/matrix-planting-garden-design); [Rootsimple: Oudolf's enhanced nature](https://www.rootsimple.com/2013/05/piet-oudolfs-enhanced-nature/)
- Recognition: he shared the Chelsea Best Garden award with Arne Maynard in 2000 — [Limelight Management: Arne Maynard biography PDF](https://www.limelightmanagement.com/uploaded_files/client/1437754386-Arne_Maynard.pdf). He also designed the Oudolf borders at RHS Wisley — [Middle-Sized Garden: 5 lessons from the Oudolf borders at Wisley](https://www.themiddlesizedgarden.co.uk/5-lessons-planting-style-new-oudolf-borders-rhs-wisley/)
- Source caution: studiomatrx.org came up for both Oudolf and Burle Marx but reads as promotional and unclear, so it was not used.

**Dan Pearson (UK)**
- Best in Show at Chelsea 2015 for the Laurent-Perrier garden, which evoked the rockery and trout stream at Chatsworth. Observers described it as an "exotic meadow garden of wild and not-so-wild flowers", without the cultivated, formal look of most show gardens. He is described as "enviably sensitive to place" — [Learning with Experts: Best in Show 2015](https://www.learningwithexperts.com/gardening/blog/the-laurent-perrier-garden-chelsea-flower-show-2015); [Dan Pearson Studio: Chatsworth Laurent-Perrier](https://danpearsonstudio.com/chatsworth-laurent-perrier/)
- Tokachi Millennium Forest, Hokkaido: a garden meant to last 1,000 years. The Meadow Garden planting "is designed to work as one entity so that it evolves in much the same way as that of a real meadow". It aims to reflect the natural geography, raise indigenous flora and evoke the "physical sensations and emotions experienced in nature". There is a book with Midori Shintani (Filbert Press) — [Dan Pearson Studio: Tokachi](https://www.danpearsonstudio.com/tokachi-millennium-forest/); [Gardens Illustrated: Tokachi, the 1,000-year garden](https://www.gardensillustrated.com/gardens/international/tokachi-millennium-forest-dan-pearson); [Filbert Press](https://www.filbertpress.com/news/tokachi-millennium-forest)
- Book *Home Ground: Sanctuary in the City* (2019) is about his own garden in Peckham, London — [UW Hort Library / Pearson sources via search](https://depts.washington.edu/hortlib/book/tokachi-millennium-forest/); [Dan Pearson Studio bio](https://danpearsonstudio.com/dan-pearson/)

**Tom Stuart-Smith (UK)**
- Gold and Best in Show at Chelsea 2008 for the Laurent-Perrier garden. His studio is known for "gardens that combine naturalism and modernity" and for using rare and unusual plants — [Crocus: Laurent-Perrier Garden 2008](https://www.crocus.co.uk/chelsea/our-history/laurent-perrier-garden-2008/); [Crocus: About Tom Stuart-Smith](https://www.crocus.co.uk/chelsea/about-tom). One source says he has won three Best in Shows, which was not confirmed elsewhere.
- His Chelsea 2024 garden for the National Garden Scheme updated the traditional cottage garden with "exuberant woodland planting" — [Gardenista: Tom Stuart-Smith 2024](https://www.gardenista.com/posts/tom-stuart-smith-2024-chelsea-flower-show)
- He calls himself a "contextualist": designers are "led by the frame and the circumstances". The Barn Garden (Hertfordshire) was made from an empty field around a barn, and its Plant Library (from 2020) shows varieties side by side. He designed the new ecological walled garden at Knepp Castle, replacing a formal layout of raised beds and a croquet lawn — [Tom Stuart-Smith: The Barn Garden](https://www.tomstuartsmith.co.uk/the-barn-garden); [Gardens Illustrated: Knepp walled garden](https://gardensillustrated.com/gardens/walled-garden-knepp-castle); [Brightwater: rise of naturalistic planting and TSS](https://www.brightwaterholidays.com/blog/the-rise-of-naturalistic-planting-and-the-gardens-of-tom-stuart-smith)

**Beth Chatto (UK, 1923–2018; Beth Chatto Gardens, Essex)**
- Principle: "right plant, right place". The Gravel Garden was an experiment in drought-tolerant planting with minimal watering. Gaps in the planting are disguised by gravel mulch, and self-seeders are encouraged (the gardens publish a list of "the 21 best self-seeders") — [Beth Chatto: Gravel garden and self-seeding plants](https://www.bethchatto.co.uk/discover/our-blog/guides/the-gravel-garden-and-self-seeding-plants.htm); [GardenAdvice: Beth Chatto style garden](https://gardenadvice.co.uk/blog/beth-chatto-style-garden/); [Garden Museum: Beth Chatto, The Gravel Garden](https://www.gardenmuseum.org.uk/?p=3118)
- Named plants: Euphorbia characias subsp. wulfenii, chosen for full sun and free-draining soil — [RHS: 10 plants Beth Chatto's head gardener couldn't do without](https://www.rhs.org.uk/garden-inspiration/plants-we-love/beth-chatto-top-10-plants). Later additions after only truly drought-tolerant plants survived include Stipa barbata, Hylotelephium 'Red Cauli' and Romneya coulteri. A Chatto sand-garden guide uses Stachys byzantina, Stipa gigantea and Stipa tenuissima — [Beth Chatto: Gravel garden creation and maintenance](https://www.bethchatto.co.uk/discover/our-blog/guides/gravel-garden-creation-and-maintenance.htm); [Beth Chatto: Chelsea-inspired sand garden](https://www.bethchatto.co.uk/discover/our-blog/guides/chelsea-inspired-sand-garden.htm)

**Gertrude Jekyll (UK, 1843–1932)**
- Her books *Colour in the Flower Garden* (1908) and *Colour Schemes for the Flower Garden* (1919) are still described as practical and inspirational. She planned some borders as a spectrum running through the rainbow, with blue and indigo kept at opposite ends so they never met. She worked in partnership with the architect Edwin Lutyens — [Project Gutenberg: Colour in the Flower Garden](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/50764/pg50764-images.html); [Country Life: best flower borders in Britain](https://www.countrylife.co.uk/gardens/country-gardens-and-gardening-tips/the-best-flower-borders-in-britain-and-how-they-work-their-magic); [Jury: Edwin Lutyens tag](https://jury.co.nz/tag/edwin-lutyens/)

**Christopher Lloyd (UK, 1921–2006; Great Dixter)**
- His legacy is the mixed border: shrubs, clumping perennials, annuals and bold colour combinations together, with beds treated as test beds for experiment. The Long Border is kept going from April into November by succession planting. Lloyd said its peak runs from mid-June to mid-August. Perovskia is hard-pruned in spring so its blue spires take over from July. He developed Tagetes 'Cinnabar' at Dixter. There is also an Exotic Garden — [Storyteller Garden: Christopher Lloyd and Great Dixter](https://storytellergarden.co.uk/great-dixter-christopher-lloyd); [Gardens Illustrated: changing border planting](https://www.gardensillustrated.com/features/changing-border-planting); [Great Dixter: The Exotic Garden](https://www.greatdixter.co.uk/the-exotic-garden)

**Monty Don (UK; BBC Gardeners' World presenter; Longmeadow, Herefordshire)**
- The Jewel Garden is "the physical and spiritual centre" of Longmeadow. Its rich "gem" colours and metallics celebrate the Dons' former costume-jewellery business. He relaid it in 2024 without rigorous planning, lifting and dividing the perennials. He promotes small colourful corners that help wildlife — [Ideal Home: inside Monty Don's garden](https://idealhome.co.uk/garden/inside-monty-don-garden-longmeadow); [Homes & Gardens: Longmeadow tour](https://www.homesandgardens.com/news/monty-don-garden-218267); [Hachette: The Jewel Garden](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/monty-don-17/the-jewel-garden/9781444718782/); [montydon.com: the garden](https://montydon.com/thegarden)
- Instagram @themontydon. Third-party estimates disagree, from 1M+ to 2M+ followers — [Search summary citing Feedspot/aggregators](https://videos.feedspot.com/uk_gardening_youtube_channels/)

**Sarah Price (UK)**
- Chelsea Golds in 2012, 2018 and 2023. The 2012 Telegraph garden had a glade of silver birch over cow parsley and native meadow flowers. The 2018 M&G garden was Mediterranean-inspired, in vibrant earth tones. The 2023 Nurture Landscapes garden drew on the paintings and garden of Cedric Morris. Her style is naturalistic and "painterly" — [Crocus: About Sarah Price](https://www.crocus.co.uk/chelsea/about-sarah); [Crocus: M&G Garden 2018](https://www.crocus.co.uk/chelsea/mandg-garden-2018); [Gardenista: Sarah Price Mediterranean garden 2018](https://www.gardenista.com/posts/chelsea-flower-show-2018-inside-look-mediterranean-garden-sarah-price); [Everand/Gardens Illustrated: Nurture Landscapes garden, Gold](https://www.everand.com/article/650302962/A-Shimmering-Return-To-Form)

**James Hitchmough and Nigel Dunnett (University of Sheffield, "Sheffield school")**
- They were the lead horticultural and planting consultants for the London 2012 Olympic Park from 2008. Hitchmough's focus is perennials (horticultural ecology) and Dunnett's is annuals. The Stadium meadows were the largest direct-sown annual meadows ever created in the UK. They coined "pictorial meadows" for intensely colourful, painterly sown meadows, and the Olympic "Gold" meadows were meant to be yellow and gold. Dunnett said they never called the non-native mixes "wildflower meadows" and described a 20-year "eco-aesthetic" approach — [Pictorial Meadows: London Olympic Park meadows](https://pictorialmeadows.co.uk/pages/london-olympic-park-meadows) (commercial source, Dunnett-founded); [Gardenista: Olympic Park, a new kind of English garden](https://www.gardenista.com/posts/olympic-park-a-new-kind-of-english-garden); [NERC Planet Earth feature](https://issuu.com/nerc/docs/pe_summer_12/10)
- Dunnett died on 26 April 2026, aged 63. He was Professor of Planting Design and Vegetation Technology, designed the planting for Sheffield's Grey to Green scheme, was a green-roof pioneer and founded Pictorial Meadows. Green Estate's CEO called him "famous for his resilient and practical urban spaces" — [University of Sheffield: In loving memory of Professor Nigel Dunnett](https://sheffield.ac.uk/architecture-landscape/news/loving-memory-professor-nigel-dunnett); [Garden Forum: Dunnett dies aged 63](https://gardenforum.co.uk/news/people/professor-nigel-dunnett-dies-aged-63); [World Landscape Architect: Remembering Nigel Dunnett](https://worldlandscapearchitect.com/remembering-nigel-dunnett/)

**Roberto Burle Marx (Brazil, 1909–1994)**
- A modernist "painter with plants" who used modernist curves and mass planting of varied form, colour and texture, largely with native Brazilian flora. His most famous project is the Copacabana promenade (Avenida Atlântica, finished 1970): wavy Portuguese-stone mosaics with groupings of native, sea-breeze-resistant trees and palms, forming a giant abstract painting in which no section repeats. His Sítio estate is a UNESCO World Heritage site, cited for fusing modernist ideas with native tropical plants and for "exuberant mass planting" — [Unitec research paper](https://www.researchbank.ac.nz/server/api/core/bitstreams/387f561e-d918-4bdb-a706-c6f0262204d7/content); [Daily Art Magazine: Burle Marx](https://www.dailyartmagazine.com/roberto-burle-marx/); [UNESCO document](https://whc.unesco.org/document/189242); [ASLA: Lessons from Burle Marx](https://www.asla.org/2025/03/13/lessons-from-roberto-burle-marx-how-to-design-resilient-urban-landscapes/)
- ASLA notes he championed native plants because they make self-sustaining ecosystems that need little intervention — [ASLA](https://www.asla.org/news-insights/dirt/lessons-from-roberto-burle-marx-how-to-design-resilient-urban-landscapes)

**Fernando Caruncho (Spain)**
- Mas de les Voltes (Empordà, Girona, 1994) first brought him to attention. It is a grid of golden wheat fields laid out as parterres and framed by olive trees and cypresses. His creed: "the primary material is the light, but the way we control it is with geometry". His work is built on geometry and light, brings agriculture into the garden and connects the garden to the wider landscape — [Galerie: Caruncho's gardens play with light and geometry](https://galeriemagazine.com/fernando-caruncho-gardens-book/); [1stDibs Introspective: Fernando Caruncho](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/fernando-caruncho); [Fernando Caruncho studio](https://web.fernandocaruncho.com/en/studio/)
- Reported death: Gardens Illustrated published "Remembering Fernando Caruncho" and a Vanitatis headline dated August 2026 read "Muere Fernando Caruncho". Neither page was read in full, so the date is unconfirmed — [Gardens Illustrated: in memory](https://www.gardensillustrated.com/features/fernando-caruncho-in-memory)

**Arne Maynard (UK)**
- He studied architecture first and is an RHS Gold medallist with a Best in Show. He shared the Chelsea Best Garden award with Piet Oudolf in 2000 and won Gold for the Laurent-Perrier Bicentenary garden in 2012. His designs rest on strong architectural frameworks of walls, hedges and topiary. He says he has used topiary "in almost all the gardens I've designed… since 1986" — [Limelight Management: Arne Maynard bio](https://www.limelightmanagement.com/uploaded_files/client/1437754386-Arne_Maynard.pdf); [EBTS: Arne Maynard honorary member](https://ebts.org/uk/2020/arne-maynard-joins-ebts-as-an-honorary-member/)
- At his own home, Allt-y-bela (Monmouthshire), formal topiary near the house gives way to loose wildflower meadows. A small north London garden combined Corten steel with clipped topiary — [Gardenista: Arne Maynard at home in Wales](https://www.gardenista.com/posts/designer-visit-arne-maynard-at-home-in-wales); [Gardens Illustrated: topiary and Corten in north London](https://gardensillustrated.com/gardens/town-and-city/topiary-steel-small-garden-arne-maynard)

**Andy Sturgeon (UK)**
- Known for "a marriage of bold architectural elements with glorious, often unusual planting". One source says seven Chelsea Golds and Best in Show in 2010 and 2016. Another Crocus page credits a Best in Show in 2019. The two may not conflict (three Best in Shows), but no source confirmed the full count — [Crocus: About Andy Sturgeon](https://www.crocus.co.uk/chelsea-blog/garden/2019/andy-sturgeon); [Andy Sturgeon Garden Design](https://www.andysturgeon.com/?p=840)

**Charlotte Harris (UK; Harris Bugg Studio)**
- Co-founded the "values-driven" Harris Bugg Studio with Hugo Bugg in 2017. She has three Chelsea Golds and the Best Show Garden award of 2023 — [Crocus: Meet the designers](https://www.crocus.co.uk/chelsea/meet-the-designers)

**Adam Frost (UK; BBC Gardeners' World presenter)**
- Seven Chelsea Gold medals. Older biographies say five, which predates his later wins. He is self-taught and started under Geoff Hamilton at Barnsdale. He was influenced by the book *Dream Plants for the Natural Garden* and by the rugged landscape where he grew up. He is called "the designers' designer" for combining design with planting, and his on-screen approach is "let's get stuck in and have a go" — [Cavac/Wikipedia mirror: Adam Frost](https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/Adam%20Frost%20%28garden%20designer%29); [Limelight: Adam Frost bio PDF](https://www.limelightmanagement.com/uploaded_files/client/1369997887-Adam_Frost.pdf); [Bognor Regis Observer: Frost's Gold garden](https://www.bognor.co.uk/news/magazine-stars-celebrate-adam-frosts-gold-medal-winning-rhs-chelsea-flower-show-garden-704242)

**Thomas Rainer and Claudia West (USA; *Planting in a Post-Wild World*, Timber Press 2015)**
- Designed plant communities: compatible species in interlocking vertical layers, so that no ground is left bare. Structural plants (trees, shrubs, tall perennials, grasses) go in first. "Design layers should be legible; functional layers should be diverse." Natives are a starting point, but ecological performance matters more than where a plant comes from. Rainer's line "there are no mulch circles in the forest" argues for ground-cover plants instead of mulch — [ASLA: Thomas Rainer, There Are No Mulch Circles in the Forest](https://www.asla.org/news-insights/dirt/thomas-rainer-there-are-no-mulch-circles-in-the-forest); [Pacific Horticulture: Designing plant communities](https://pacifichorticulture.org/articles/designing-plant-communities/); [Greenhouse Mag review](https://www.greenhousemag.com/news/planting-post-wild-world-claudia-west-thomas-rainer)

**Luciano Giubbilei (Italy/UK)**
- Gold and Best in Show at Chelsea 2014 for the Laurent-Perrier garden, his third Chelsea Gold. It balanced intricacy and simplicity, was called "contemplative", and departed from historic Italian symmetry. Gardens Illustrated said it "captured English romanticism with carefully controlled planting and colour". Fergus Garrett gave him an experimental border at Great Dixter, recorded in his book *The Art of Making Gardens* — [Crocus: Laurent-Perrier Garden 2014](https://www.crocus.co.uk/chelsea/our-history/laurent-perrier-garden-2014/); [Learning with Experts: Best in Show 2014](https://www.learningwithexperts.com/blogs/articles/best-in-show-chelsea-flower-show-2014-luciano-giubbileis-laurent-perrier-garden); [The Gardener's Eye: Giubbilei border at Great Dixter](https://thegardenerseye.blogspot.com/2015/06/luciano-giubbileis-border-at-great.html); [1stDibs: Luciano Giubbilei](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/luciano-giubbilei)
- No source checked called him "minimalist", although that label is common in the press.

**Jinny Blom (UK)**
- London practice since 2000. She co-designed the Healing Garden with the then Prince of Wales at Chelsea 2002, won Chelsea Gold in 2007 for Laurent-Perrier, and designed the Sentebale garden for Prince Harry's charity in 2013. Gardens Illustrated published her "50 invaluable plants" — [Garden Design: Jinny Blom](https://www.gardendesign.com/designers/jinny-blom.html); [1stDibs: Jinny Blom](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/jinny-blom); [Gardens Illustrated: 50 invaluable plants, Jinny Blom](https://gardensillustrated.com/plants/great-plants-jinny-blom); [Gardenista: 100th Chelsea](https://www.gardenista.com/posts/the-inside-scoop-on-the-100th-annual-chelsea-flower-show/)

**Olivier Filippi (France; nursery and Le Jardin Sec, near Montpellier)**
- Books: *The Dry Gardening Handbook* and *Planting Design for Dry Gardens* (Filbert Press, 2016). Method: a mineral (gravel or pebble) mulch keeps plant crowns dry, suppresses weeds and reflects light as rocky ground does. Plants get one deep soak at autumn planting, and from year two the garden lives on rain alone. Poor stony soil is an asset because weeds are suppressed. Thyme and Stachys byzantina are used as ground covers woven between taller plants. Summer-dormant plants should not be watered, since "reviving" them in August kills them. Ground-cover alternatives to lawn are central to his work — [Filbert Press: Planting Design for Dry Gardens](https://www.filbertpress.com/dry-garden) (publisher page); [Pacific Horticulture: Planting design for dry gardens](https://pacifichorticulture.org/articles/planting-design-dry-gardens); [Gardens Illustrated: Le Jardin Sec](https://www.gardensillustrated.com/gardens/international/jardin-sec-filippi-gravel-garden-france); [Noel Kingsbury: Filippi and the Mediterranean garden of the future](https://www.noelkingsbury.com/noelsgarden-blog/2019/1/11/olivier-filippi-and-the-mediterranean-garden-of-the-future)

**Ron Finley (USA; "Gangsta Gardener", South Central LA)**
- He planted vegetables on the parkway strip outside his house, which led to a TED talk, "A Guerrilla Gardener in South Central LA". Wikipedia dates it 2013 with 3M+ views; Kinfolk dates it 2012 with 3.5M+ views. He founded The Ron Finley Project, a non-profit urban-gardening training facility, and released a 10-lesson MasterClass, "Ron Finley Teaches Gardening", on Earth Day 2020 — [Kinfolk](https://www.kinfolk.com/?p=262576); [MasterClass: Ron Finley](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/ron-finleys-community-gardening-activism); [LA Weekly](https://www.laweekly.com/?p=444404); [NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/181691507)

- His *Cha No Niwa – Japanese Tea Garden* won Best in Show and RHS Garden of the Year at Chelsea 2025. It was the first garden to win both the People's Choice award and Best in Show, and his first Main Avenue garden after 17 smaller Chelsea gardens — [Gardens Illustrated: 2025 Best in Show](https://www.gardensillustrated.com/chelsea/cha-no-niwa-japanese-tea-garden-kazuyuki-ishihara); [Gardens Illustrated: People's Choice 2025](https://www.gardensillustrated.com/chelsea/peoples-choice-award-2025); [RHS: 2025 awards](https://www.rhs.org.uk/shows-events/rhs-chelsea-flower-show/history/2025/awards)

### Inferences
These are labelled as general horticultural knowledge or as interpretation, and were not verified in this session. Use them as starting points for prompts.

- **Oudolf lens.** Palette: Sporobolus heterolepis, Molinia caerulea, Deschampsia, Calamagrostis 'Karl Foerster', Echinacea, Monarda, Astrantia, Eryngium, Veronicastrum, Persicaria amplexicaulis, Sanguisorba, Salvia nemorosa, Phlomis russeliana, Eupatorium. Visual hallmarks: knee-to-chest-height drifts and repeated clumps in a grassy matrix; brown and silver seedheads and skeletons left standing over winter; buttons, spires and umbels contrasted; a muted plum, pink and buff palette. Suits: medium to large sunny sites and clients who accept a "dead" winter look. The High Line (NYC), Hauser & Wirth Somerset and Lurie Garden (Chicago) are his best-known public works.
- **Dan Pearson lens.** Soft, intimate naturalism. Woodland edges, meadow-like perennials, umbellifers, Japanese and woodland influence, mown paths through long grass. Suits: rural or semi-rural plots and clients who value atmosphere over neatness.
- **Tom Stuart-Smith lens.** Hornbeam or yew blocks and clipped forms as a rhythm against billowing perennials and grasses (the 2008 Chelsea garden used copper-beech and hornbeam cubes with Iris, Anthriscus and Geum). Rectangular water tanks. Suits: larger country gardens. "Contextualist" means the lens should first read the existing setting.
- **Chatto lens.** No lawn, gravel ground plane, plants in naturalistic islands with gravel "rivers" between. Silver, grey-green and acid-yellow (Euphorbia) palette, verbascum and allium spires, stipa haze. The best-documented low-water, low-maintenance model in a temperate climate.
- **Jekyll lens.** Long double herbaceous borders against old brick walls or yew hedges. Cool-to-hot-to-cool colour gradation. Lutyens-style terraces, stone steps, rills and pergolas. High maintenance (staking, deadheading). Mostly a "heritage" or "romantic" lens.
- **Lloyd and Great Dixter lens.** Dense, clashing colour (magenta with orange), mixed borders that include shrubs, meadow areas in long grass, an exotic garden of cannas, bananas and dahlias. Visually busy and lush. High maintenance.
- **Monty Don lens.** Box-edged or hedged "rooms" at Longmeadow; Jewel Garden of deep reds, oranges, purples and metallic bronze foliage; productive vegetable plots; wildlife-friendly. An approachable "British TV" lens.
- **Sheffield lens.** Sown pictorial meadows (cornflower, poppy, Coreopsis, Cosmos in annual mixes); urban rain gardens and green roofs; drought-resilient "steppe" and prairie perennial mixes sown rather than planted. Suits budget-conscious large areas and verges.
- **Burle Marx lens.** Sinuous bands of single-species mass planting in strong colour blocks (bromeliads, philodendron, heliconia, palms); black-and-white wave-pattern mosaic paving; abstract free-form pools. Needs a tropical or subtropical climate, or a "translation" using hardy bold-leaf plants.
- **Caruncho lens.** Grids, parterres of grass or wheat, clipped hedges in long parallel lines, still reflecting pools, olive and cypress, a near-monochrome green and gold palette, and strong light and shadow. Suits large sunny sites and minimalist clients. Low plant diversity makes maintenance mostly clipping.
- **Maynard lens.** Clipped topiary domes, cones and buns (yew, box or substitutes) near the house, dissolving into long-grass meadow with mown paths. Natural materials such as oak and stone. Suits period or country houses. Topiary needs annual clipping.
- **Sturgeon and Harris lenses.** Contemporary show-garden modernism: bold hard landscaping (stone, steel, timber screens) with lush, textured, often drought-tolerant planting.
- **Frost lens.** Approachable, naturalistic family gardens: stone, timber, water features, wildlife planting.
- **Rainer and West lens.** Dense ground-cover layers instead of mulch, sedges (Carex) as matrix, native North American perennials. A good ecological lens for US clients.
- **Giubbilei lens.** Restraint: clipped hedges, gravel and stone, carefully limited colour, contemplative and Italianate-modern.
- **Blom lens.** Lush, romantic, context-driven planting within architectural structure.
- **Filippi lens.** Mediterranean garrigue: cushion shrubs (Cistus, lavender, Phlomis, Santolina, Euphorbia), ground-cover "lawns" of thyme and Achillea, pebble mulch. Low water.
- **Finley lens.** Productive and community-focused rather than a design aesthetic: edibles in reclaimed or front-yard space. Better used as "productive" than as a visual style.

## Q3. Recurring style families: defining traits and exemplars

### Takeaway
Nine families map cleanly onto the designers above. Each has a distinct, renderable "look". Several designers bridge families: Maynard is formal plus meadow, Stuart-Smith is modern plus naturalistic, and Price is naturalistic plus Mediterranean.

### Cited Findings
- **Naturalistic / New Perennial.** Grass matrix, repeated structural perennials, life-cycle and seedhead interest, little attention to colour. Exemplars: Oudolf, Pearson, Stuart-Smith, Rainer and West, Price — [Hortmag](https://www.hortmag.com/gardens/matrix-planting-garden-design); [Brightwater: rise of naturalistic planting](https://www.brightwaterholidays.com/blog/the-rise-of-naturalistic-planting-and-the-gardens-of-tom-stuart-smith); [ASLA: Rainer](https://www.asla.org/news-insights/dirt/thomas-rainer-there-are-no-mulch-circles-in-the-forest)
- **Ecological / sown meadow (Sheffield school).** Sown, "pictorial", eco-aesthetic, urban-resilient. Exemplars: Dunnett and Hitchmough — [Pictorial Meadows](https://pictorialmeadows.co.uk/pages/london-olympic-park-meadows)
- **Cottage / romantic / colourist border.** Colour-graded or clashing herbaceous and mixed borders with succession. Exemplars: Jekyll, Lloyd, Don, Stuart-Smith's 2024 "updated cottage garden" — [Country Life borders](https://www.countrylife.co.uk/gardens/country-gardens-and-gardening-tips/the-best-flower-borders-in-britain-and-how-they-work-their-magic); [Storyteller Garden](https://storytellergarden.co.uk/great-dixter-christopher-lloyd); [Gardenista TSS 2024](https://www.gardenista.com/posts/tom-stuart-smith-2024-chelsea-flower-show)
- **Formal / classical / topiary.** Architectural frameworks of walls, hedges and topiary. Exemplars: Maynard, Caruncho, Giubbilei — [Limelight: Maynard](https://www.limelightmanagement.com/uploaded_files/client/1437754386-Arne_Maynard.pdf); [Galerie: Caruncho](https://galeriemagazine.com/fernando-caruncho-gardens-book/)
- **Modern minimalist / contemporary.** Geometry, light, restraint, bold hard landscaping. Exemplars: Caruncho, Giubbilei, Sturgeon, Harris — [Crocus: Sturgeon](https://www.crocus.co.uk/chelsea-blog/garden/2019/andy-sturgeon); [Crocus: Giubbilei 2014](https://www.crocus.co.uk/chelsea/our-history/laurent-perrier-garden-2014/)
- **Mediterranean / dry / gravel.** Mineral mulch, no irrigation after establishment, no lawn, ground covers. Exemplars: Filippi, Chatto, Price (2018) — [Filbert Press](https://www.filbertpress.com/dry-garden); [Beth Chatto gravel garden](https://www.bethchatto.co.uk/discover/our-blog/guides/gravel-garden-creation-and-maintenance.htm); [Gardenista: Price 2018](https://www.gardenista.com/posts/chelsea-flower-show-2018-inside-look-mediterranean-garden-sarah-price)
- **Japanese-inspired.** Exemplar: Kazuyuki Ishihara (2025 Chelsea Best in Show tea garden); Pearson's Tokachi has Japanese context — [Gardens Illustrated: Ishihara](https://www.gardensillustrated.com/chelsea/cha-no-niwa-japanese-tea-garden-kazuyuki-ishihara)
- **Tropical / Burle Marx modernism.** Curving mass-planted colour bands, native tropical flora, mosaic paving. Exemplar: Burle Marx — [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/document/189242); [Unitec paper](https://www.researchbank.ac.nz/server/api/core/bitstreams/387f561e-d918-4bdb-a706-c6f0262204d7/content)
- **Productive / kitchen garden.** Exemplars: Dowding (no dig), Don (Longmeadow vegetable garden), Finley (urban food), Caruncho (agriculture as garden) — [Yorkshire Post: Dowding](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/lifestyle/homes-and-gardens/whos-charles-dowding-everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-gardening-youtuber-2670812); [MasterClass: Finley](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/ron-finleys-community-gardening-activism); [1stDibs: Caruncho](https://www.1stdibs.com/introspective-magazine/fernando-caruncho)
- **Wildlife / rewilding.** Exemplars: Stuart-Smith's Knepp walled garden ("ecological aesthetic"), Kate Stuart-Smith's no-spray, self-seeding Serge Hill, Pearson's Tokachi — [Gardens Illustrated: Knepp](https://gardensillustrated.com/gardens/walled-garden-knepp-castle); [Country Life: Kate Stuart-Smith](https://www.countrylife.co.uk/gardens/country-gardens-and-gardening-tips/kate-stuart-smith-and-the-creation-of-her-effortlessly-romantic-english-country-garden-257352)

## Q4. Approaches suited to a retirement-age homeowner wanting a manageable but beautiful garden

### Takeaway
The best-supported low-input approaches are (1) Chatto- or Filippi-style gravel and dry gardens, with no watering after establishment, mulch that suppresses weeds and no lawn to mow; (2) Rainer and West-style dense ground-cover plant communities, with no bare soil and less weeding; and (3) a simple formal frame with a few repeated perennials. Add accessibility features: raised beds at about 71–81 cm (28–32 in), wide non-slip paths, seating and shade. Avoid high-input lenses such as Jekyll and Lloyd borders, extensive topiary, annual bedding and large lawns, unless the client has help.

### Cited Findings
- Chatto's Gravel Garden was created to test minimal watering, and gravel mulch disguises gaps — [GardenAdvice: Chatto style](https://gardenadvice.co.uk/blog/beth-chatto-style-garden/); [Beth Chatto: gravel garden maintenance](https://www.bethchatto.co.uk/discover/our-blog/guides/gravel-garden-creation-and-maintenance.htm)
- Filippi: mineral mulch suppresses weeds, and from year two the garden lives on rain alone (in a Mediterranean climate) — [Filbert Press](https://www.filbertpress.com/dry-garden)
- Rainer and West: interlocking layers so the ground is never bare, ground covers instead of mulch — [ASLA: No Mulch Circles](https://www.asla.org/news-insights/dirt/thomas-rainer-there-are-no-mulch-circles-in-the-forest)
- Burle Marx's native mass planting was praised for "self-sustaining ecosystems that required minimal intervention" — [ASLA](https://www.asla.org/2025/03/13/lessons-from-roberto-burle-marx-how-to-design-resilient-urban-landscapes/)
- The RHS is reported, second-hand, to suggest that older gardeners who struggle with mowing replace the lawn with patio or decking. An RHS Hampton Court show garden for retirees used raised beds and wider paths for wheelchair and scooter access — [Helping Hands: low-maintenance garden for the elderly](https://www.helpinghandshomecare.co.uk/care-advice/creating-a-low-maintenance-garden-for-the-elderly/); [Able Magazine: designer and retirement specialist tips](https://ablemagazine.co.uk/?p=29271)
- Raised-bed height: 71–81 cm (28–32 in) puts soil at desk height so the gardener works with the arms rather than bending the back; 46–61 cm (18–24 in) suits wheelchair users. Built-in seating is recommended — [Gardening Know How: future-proof your garden](https://www.gardeningknowhow.com/garden-how-to/info/future-proof-garden-for-aging); [AARP: accessible gardening tips](https://www.aarp.org/home-living/accessible-gardening-tips/)
- Other advice: avoid plants that spread, need heavy trimming or attract pests; avoid cobbles (trip hazard); use grippy decking; avoid narrow paths; keep tools close; provide water, shade and rest spots — [Home Instead: low-maintenance gardens](https://www.homeinstead.co.uk/care/domiciliary/help-housekeeping/low-maintenance-gardening/); [Age UK Mobility: limited mobility tips](https://www.ageukmobility.co.uk/information-advice/mobility-news/article/gardening-tips-for-those-with-limited-mobility/)
- Dowding's no-dig method is widely promoted as reducing labour (digging) for productive beds — [Yorkshire Post: Dowding](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/lifestyle/homes-and-gardens/whos-charles-dowding-everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-gardening-youtuber-2670812)
- Caveat: most accessibility sources are care-company, retailer or blog content, and their measurements vary.

### Inferences
- Recommended lenses for this client: "Chatto gravel garden" (temperate, free-draining soil); "Filippi Mediterranean" (warm, dry climates); "Oudolf-lite", a simplified matrix of a few long-lived, clump-forming perennials and grasses cut back once a year in late winter; "Maynard-lite", a few evergreen clipped forms plus meadow areas mown twice a year; and "Dowding no-dig raised beds" for clients who want to grow food.
- A modest maintenance profile per lens would make a useful AI output. Rough guide (unverified estimate): gravel or dry garden and ground-cover community are low; naturalistic matrix is low to medium (one annual cut-back); formal topiary is medium (one or two clips a year); cottage or colourist border is high (staking, deadheading, dividing); kitchen garden is medium to high.
- Design moves for an older client that are easy to render: widen paths to wheelchair width with a firm, even surface; add a bench at a focal point; replace part of the lawn with gravel planting; raise one productive bed to about 75 cm; keep tall plants away from paths; add lighting and handrails at steps.

### Gaps
- No primary RHS page on gardening in later life was retrieved; RHS advice is cited second-hand.
- No designer-authored guide specifically on "retirement gardens" was found (for example, nothing from Adam Frost or Monty Don on downsizing).
- The low-maintenance claims for naturalistic planting depend on climate and soil and were not tested against independent maintenance-hours data.
