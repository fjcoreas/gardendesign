## 1. Standard stages of a residential garden design job (first contact to aftercare)

### Takeaway
Professional practice follows a broadly consistent sequence, with stage names varying by firm: initial consultation and written brief, then measured survey and site analysis, then concept (zoning or bubble diagram, mood board), then developed master plan (hardscape layout), then planting plan and schedule, then construction details and tender, then implementation, then aftercare. UK firms often say they follow SGD "work stages" modelled on architects' stages. US practice (APLD, extension services) follows the same logic: client interview, site inventory and analysis, base plan, concept, preliminary design, master plan.

### Cited Findings
**Overall stage frameworks**
- One UK studio says its process "normally follows the work stages... very similar to architectural work stages and are as published by the Society of Garden Designers", and that "not all projects require all stages". Its stages run from outline proposals through construction and planting details to tender documentation, with planting plans and plant procurement handled alongside tender. — [Stephen Woodhams Garden Design](https://www.stephenwoodhams.com/design-process)
- Some UK firms use the Landscape Institute's stage numbering (Inception through Completion) instead of SGD labels. — [Davis Landscape Architecture](https://davisla.com/landscape-architects-work-stages/)
- The APLD definition of landscape design lists these activities: site reconnaissance, inventory, assessment and evaluation; site analysis; consultation; client interviews; and further development stages. — [APLD, Landscape Design Definition](https://www.apld.org/landscape-design-definition)
- APLD's sustainability checklist tells designers to "discuss your clients' needs and desires" and to perform a site analysis that includes soil type, watershed and drainage evaluation. — [APLD Design Sustainability checklist (PDF)](https://apld.memberclicks.net/assets/Sustainability/Design%20Sustainability.pdf)
- A typical US sequence: client consultation and interview, then site inventory and analysis, then base plan, then concept and preliminary design, then master plan, with client feedback at each step. Site analysis "may be accomplished together with the preparation of the base plan." — synthesis of US extension and landscape architecture sources returned for the APLD query, incl. [UF/IFAS EP375](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP375) and [World Landscape Architect, stages of a project](https://worldlandscapearchitect.com/practice-stages-of-a-landscape-architecture-design-project/)

**Stage 1: First contact, consultation and brief**
- The first meeting is "normally at the project site". — [World Landscape Architect](https://worldlandscapearchitect.com/practice-stages-of-a-landscape-architecture-design-project/)
- John Brookes' studio: "a meeting at your house will ascertain your requirements and enable an inspection and assessment of your garden", followed by a survey and design proposal. — [John Brookes, process](https://www.johnbrookes.com/process.asp)
- After the site meeting, designers produce a written brief for discussion, described as "a vital document upon which the design is based". One UK firm sends a written summary of the brief together with a breakdown of the design process and fees. — search summary of UK designer process pages, e.g. [Lucy Roberts, Garden Design Process](https://www.lucyroberts.uk.com/process.html), [Christine Facer, Design Process](https://www.christinefacer.com/copy-of-design-process)
- One UK guide describes a five-stage process that starts with a one-page brief. — [Garden UK, Working with a Garden Designer: UK Process](https://gardenuk.co.uk/garden-design/working-with-garden-designer-process-uk/)
- Many studios send a pre-visit questionnaire before the first meeting. — [Melissa Morton Garden Design, pre-visit questionnaire](https://melissamorton.co.uk/garden-design-questionnaire/); [Emily Hillier Garden Design, your garden questions](https://www.emilyhilliergardendesign.com/your-garden-questions); [Anna McArthur, questionnaire](https://www.annamcarthur.co.uk/garden-questions/)
- Experts advise balancing thoroughness against overwhelming the client. Some designers re-ask skipped questions in person, so a questionnaire does not need to be exhaustive. — [Houzz Pro Learn, client questionnaire guide](https://pro.houzz.co.uk/pro-learn/blog/startup-guide-interior-design-how-to-write-client-questionnaire-with-template)

**Stage 2: Survey and site analysis.** Detail is in section 2.
- The measured survey records levels, boundaries, drainage and tree positions, output as a scaled drawing at 1:50 or 1:100. — [Garden UK](https://gardenuk.co.uk/garden-design/working-with-garden-designer-process-uk/)
- Complex or sloping sites may need a specialist topographical survey. — [Your Garden Design](https://www.yourgardendesign.co.uk/design.html); [Landsberg Gardens](https://www.landsberggardens.com/services)
- Homeowners are often asked to contribute to the cost of a professional survey. — search summary of UK process pages ([Garden UK](https://gardenuk.co.uk/garden-design/working-with-garden-designer-process-uk/))
- US forms ask the client for the current legal property survey, plus site-specific zoning and building-code research. — [Jotform design questionnaire example](https://form.jotform.com/212343687298062)

**Stage 3: Concept design**
- A concept sketch "shows the layout without committing to specific materials or planting" and marks zones. One firm calls this stage the "bubble diagram". — search summary of UK process pages incl. [Garden Ninja, garden design process](https://gardenninja.co.uk/garden-design-process)
- Concept-stage deliverables at one firm: "a Concept Plan of the Garden, a Mood Board, a 3D Impression to aid visualisation and sample hard landscaping materials". — [garden design package search summary; see Ivy Hill Garden Design](https://www.ivyhillgardendesign.co.uk/create-your-garden) / [South Hams Garden Design, Outline Design](https://www.southhamsgardendesign.co.uk/outline-design)
- The concept "serves as a discussion document, allowing for modifications and refinements based on your feedback". — [Compass Garden Design, process explained](https://www.compassgardendesign.co.uk/the-garden-design-process-explained/) / [Oak and Stone Gardens, concepts](https://oakandstonegardens.co.uk/garden-design-concepts/)

**Stage 4: Developed design and master plan (layout and hardscape)**
- The master plan is a final scaled, annotated drawing with short-form specifications and construction notes. Contractors need it to price and build the scheme. — [Sitescape Design](https://sitescapedesign.com/about-us)
- "A master plan is the final annotated 2D plan providing an overview of the entire site." — UK process search summary ([Andy Stedman Design, our process](https://www.andystedmandesign.com/our-process/))

**Stage 5: Planting plan**
- The planting plan shows positions of trees, shrubs and perennials, usually with a short planting specification. — [Sitescape Design](https://sitescapedesign.com/about-us); [Kate Paterson Garden Design](https://www.katepatersongardendesign.com/process)
- It should show plant symbols and a legend listing common and botanical names, quantities and spacing. — garden design package search summary ([Elite Service Solutions, what's in a garden design package](https://www.eliteservicesolutions.co.uk/blog/whats-actually-included-in-garden-design-package/))

**Stage 6: Construction details, tender, implementation**
- The master plan and specification can be used to invite landscape contractors to bid. — [Silva Landscapes](https://silvalandscapes.com/design-process/); [Your Garden Design](https://www.yourgardendesign.co.uk/design.html)
- Construction drawings, lighting plans and planting plans are produced "if required". Designers also "provide guidance and supervision throughout the construction process". — UK process search summary ([Garden UK](https://gardenuk.co.uk/garden-design/working-with-garden-designer-process-uk/))

**Stage 7: Aftercare**
- Firms typically hand over a maintenance plan, one described as "clearly laid out and tailored to your requirements". Maintenance should be raised early in the design conversation to reduce later revisions. — [Butler and Parker, process](https://www.butlerandparker.com/process); [iScapeit, helping clients understand designs](https://www.iscapeit.com/blog/how-landscape-pros-can-help-clients-understand-design-ideas-faster)
- Questions to settle with the client: how long until the garden should look mature, and whether there is a plan for pruning or replacing plants later. — [Swanson's Nursery, Garden Design 101](https://swansonsnursery.com/blog/garden-design-101)

### Inferences
- A workable 8-step method for the AI assistant mirrors the professional sequence: (1) intake and photos, (2) client interview to produce a written brief, (3) site analysis from photos plus answers, (4) zoning or bubble concept with two or three options, (5) chosen layout as a master plan, (6) planting plan with schedule, (7) budget, phasing and implementation order, (8) maintenance and aftercare calendar. Professionals send the brief back for sign-off before designing. The assistant should do the same by summarising the brief and asking "Have I got this right?"
- "Not all projects require all stages" (SGD-style). For a parent's garden on a side-project budget, the tender and construction-detail stages can shrink to a DIY or contractor-ready summary.

## 2. What goes into a site analysis, and what can be inferred from photos versus asked

### Takeaway
A professional site appraisal records: aspect and orientation; sun and shade patterns; soil type, pH and depth; drainage; levels and slope; microclimate (frost pockets, rain shadows, sun traps, wind tunnels); exposure and prevailing wind; existing plants and structures (keep, move or remove); services above and below ground; good and bad views; privacy and overlooking; access. Photos reveal structure, plants, views, levels and condition well. Compass orientation, climate zone, soil chemistry, drainage behaviour, wind, services and seasonal changes mostly have to be asked or looked up.

### Cited Findings
**Checklist content (RHS Level 2/3 syllabus-derived course notes and professional checklists)**
- The RHS Level 2 syllabus item asks students to "state what needs to be recorded when carrying out a site appraisal, including soil type, contour, aspect, micro climate, exposure and drainage." — [Hortigraph, RHS R2111 2.1](https://hortigraph.uk/rhs/r-2111-2-1/)
- **Soil:** record pH, acid to alkaline. pH is hard to change, so it constrains what can be planted. Soil depth matters: if it is too shallow, use raised beds. Take several samples, since soil "will not be uniform" across a site. — RHS-course-derived notes via [Hortigraph](https://hortigraph.uk/rhs/r-2111-2-1/) and [RHS Level 3 study notes](https://tentativeplantscientist.wordpress.com/2015/03/14/rhs-level-3-planning-construction-and-planting-understanding-survey-techniques-and-garden-principles/)
- **Drainage test:** dig a hole at least 0.5 m deep, fill it with water and time how long it takes to drain (a percolation test). — [Konzept Garden, site analysis checklist](https://konzeptgarden.com/blogs/news/landscape-site-analysis-checklist); [Aspire site analysis checklist](https://www.youraspire.com/templates/landscaping/site-analysis-checklist)
- **Microclimate:** surveys record "frost pockets, heavy shade, rain shadows, sun traps, wind tunnels". Aspect also includes shade and shelter. — RHS course notes ([Hortigraph](https://hortigraph.uk/rhs/r-2111-2-1/); [Brainscape, site appraisals and surveys](https://brainscape.com/flashcards/5site-appraisals-and-surveys-11363900/packs/20088165))
- **Views:** record good and bad views. "Borrowed views can be used to create a larger garden or it may be necessary to hide ugly views by screening." — RHS course notes ([Hortigraph](https://hortigraph.uk/rhs/r-2111-2-1/))
- **Services:** record overhead and underground services. Underground services (drainage, water, sewage, gas, oil, electricity, cable TV) "may limit where excavations can be safely made." — RHS course notes ([Hortigraph](https://hortigraph.uk/rhs/r-2111-2-1/))
- **US extension site-inventory list:** sun/shade patterns, plantings, views, drainage, utilities, structures. — [UF/IFAS Developing Your Garden Plan (PDF)](https://sfyl.ifas.ufl.edu/media/sfylifasufledu/wakulla/images/master-gardeners/Developing-Your-Garden-Plan.pdf); [UF/IFAS EP375](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP375)
- Other checklists add site topography, subsoil, sun direction, local climate and prevailing wind. — [Chelsea Green, 15 questions](https://www.chelseagreen.com/blog/15-questions-design-garden/)
- **Existing plants:** weigh the health and age of existing plants, then decide whether to keep, prune or remove them. Sandy, clay or acidic soil may call for amendments or different plant choices. — [Fine Gardening, 6 Questions Garden Designers Ask](https://www.finegardening.com/article/6-questions-garden-designers-ask-before-they-plan-a-new-garden). Check whether existing plants can be relocated instead of removed. — [UF/IFAS Gardening Solutions, 10 planning tips](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/design/ten-planning-tips-for-design/)
- **Light:** observing light across the whole day, and ideally across seasons, is essential. — [Fine Gardening](https://www.finegardening.com/article/6-questions-garden-designers-ask-before-they-plan-a-new-garden)
- **Base map:** "Before you design, inventory what you have... draw an accurate base map of your site", recording permanent features and measurements. — [garden.org](https://garden.org/learn/articles/view/1353/)

**Aspect, sun and hardiness reference data**
- In the UK, south-facing gardens get the most direct sun, often 6+ hours in summer. North-facing gardens often get under 2 hours of direct sun ([Flourish Landscaping, orientation guide](https://flourishlandscaping.co.uk/garden-advice/garden-orientation-guide/)), or 2 to 4 hours per another source ([Garden UK, aspect explained](https://gardenuk.co.uk/garden-design/garden-aspect-orientation-explained-uk/)). Figures vary by source.
- Common sun-map categories: full sun 6+ hours, partial shade 3 to 6 hours, deep shade under 3 hours. — [You Should Grow, sun exposure](https://youshouldgrow.com/sun-exposure/); [Garden Ninja, aspects explained](https://www.gardenninja.co.uk/garden-aspects-explained/)
- Method: stand at the back wall of the house with a compass to find which way the garden faces, then track sun in the sunniest and shadiest parts across the day. — [Garden Ninja](https://www.gardenninja.co.uk/garden-aspects-explained/); [Gardening Beyond](https://gardeningbeyond.com/how-to-know-which-way-your-garden-faces/)
- **RHS hardiness ratings (since 2012):** H1a (>15°C, heated glasshouse), H1b (10 to 15), H1c (5 to 10), H2 (1 to 5), H3 (-5 to 1), H4 (-10 to -5), H5 (-15 to -10), H6 (-20 to -15), H7 (< -20°C). — [RHS hardiness rating](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/trials-awards/award-of-garden-merit/rhs-hardiness-rating); [Gardens Illustrated, hardiness ratings explained](https://www.gardensillustrated.com/plants/plant-hardiness-ratings-explained)
- **USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, 2023 edition:** 13 zones based on average annual extreme minimum temperature, using 1991 to 2020 data (previous edition 1976 to 2005). It is on average about 2.5°F warmer than the previous map across the contiguous US. — [USDA ARS announcement](https://www.ars.usda.gov/news-events/news/research-news/2023/usda-unveils-updated-plant-hardiness-zone-map/); [Oregon State University](https://news.oregonstate.edu/news/new-plant-hardiness-map-used-gardeners-nationwide-and-based-osu-climate-data-unveiled)
- The western US also uses the Sunset climate zone system alongside USDA zones. — [Homes & Gardens, US hardiness zones and Sunset system](https://www.homesandgardens.com/gardens/us-hardiness-zones-sunset-system)

**Mediterranean-climate specifics**
- Mediterranean-type climates (Mediterranean basin, California, parts of Australia, Chile, South Africa) have hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters. Mediterranean plants need dry crowns in winter, so drainage is critical. Overwatering does more damage than underwatering. Hydrozoning and gravel or mineral mulch are standard tools. — [Gardenia.net, Mediterranean gardens](https://www.gardenia.net/guide/mediterranean-gardens-designs-plant-lists-care); [Gardenia.net, drought-tolerant design](https://www.gardenia.net/guide/drought-tolerant-garden-design-that-looks-lush-and-thrives) (commercial guide)
- Establishment: water deeply about once a week through the first summer, then taper off. — [Dragonfly Farm, Mediterranean drought-tolerant garden](https://dragonflyfarmlanglois.com/blogs/garden-guides/how-to-plan-a-mediterranean-drought-tolerant-garden) (commercial)
- In fire-prone areas, use low-growing, fire-resistant plants near structures, and follow the local fire agency's defensible-space and mulch rules. — [Gardenly, California native garden](https://gardenly.app/garden-styles/native-plant-garden/california) (commercial app guide; verify with local fire authority, e.g. CAL FIRE)

### Inferences (photo-inferable versus must-ask: my analysis, not sourced)
**Usually inferable from good photos** (wide shots from house and from garden end, plus close-ups):
- Overall layout, rough dimensions (with a known-size reference such as a door, fence panel or paving slab), boundaries and their materials and condition
- Existing hardscape (patio, paths, steps, walls, sheds), and its condition and style
- Visible slope and level changes, steps and retaining walls
- Existing trees, shrubs, lawn and beds, broad plant identification and health, mature trees worth keeping
- Good views to frame, bad views to screen (neighbours' windows, bins, sheds), and overlooking or privacy gaps
- House architecture, materials and style (to tie the garden to the house)
- Shadow patterns at the time of the photo (only a snapshot; time and date needed to interpret)
- Visible services: manholes, drain covers, outdoor taps, overhead cables, AC units, meters
- Signs of problems: moss or waterlogged lawn (poor drainage or shade), bare soil under trees (dry shade), wind-sculpted plants (exposure)

**Needs to be asked, measured or looked up:**
- Compass orientation (which way the back of the house faces). Ask, or derive from address or a map. This is the single most important missing fact.
- Location and climate: town or postcode to get hardiness zone (USDA, RHS rating, Sunset), rainfall, frost dates, coastal or inland, elevation
- Soil type and pH: ask what grows well locally (rhododendrons and camellias thriving suggests acid soil), or suggest a cheap pH kit and squeeze test
- Drainage behaviour: does water sit after rain, are there boggy spots? Suggest the hole test above.
- Wind exposure and prevailing wind direction; frost pockets
- How sun changes by season: does the patio get sun in the evenings, is it shaded in winter?
- Underground services and septic or soakaway locations, plus legal constraints (tree preservation orders, HOA rules, conservation area, fire-zone rules, water restrictions)
- Exact measurements, if a plan to scale is wanted
- Plant names the photos cannot resolve, and what the client values sentimentally

### Gaps
- No authoritative source was found on what a designer can reliably infer from photos alone. The list above is my reasoning.
- I could not access the RHS's own site-appraisal guidance directly. RHS-derived points come from course-note sites.

## 3. Questions professional designers ask in the first consultation (consolidated questionnaire)

### Takeaway
Published questionnaires from UK studios, US firms and trade bodies converge on the same themes: who uses the garden and how; style and feel; likes and dislikes about the current garden; plants loved, hated or allergenic; maintenance time and skill, and who will do it; budget and whether it can be phased; timeframe; accessibility now and in future; wildlife and sustainability; what must stay; and site problems. Brookes' foundational principle is that design is driven first by how the occupants will use the garden.

### Cited Findings
**Philosophy**
- John Brookes held that a garden's design should be based "first and foremost on the needs of its occupants" and how people will use it. In *Room Outside* (1969) he showed that different age groups need different features. — [Wikipedia, John Brookes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brookes_(landscape_designer)); [Houzz, revisiting Brookes](https://www.houzz.com/magazine/let-s-revisit-some-revolutionary-garden-thinking-stsetivw-vs~12818891)
- Brookes' own prompts include "Who will look after the garden?" — [Blackberry Garden, John Brookes answers The Questions](https://www.blackberrygarden.co.uk/2015/06/john-brookes-mbe-answers-questions.html)

**Use, lifestyle and users**
- "Who is the garden for?" If it is for the client, decide how the space will be used: gardening only, or hosting cookouts and games. — [Fine Gardening](https://www.finegardening.com/article/6-questions-garden-designers-ask-before-they-plan-a-new-garden)
- Ask who will use the garden: adults, children (with ages), pets (type and behaviour). Is a dedicated children's area needed? — [My Garden Design, building a brief](https://mygardendesign.co.uk/building-a-garden-design-brief); [Garden Myths Landscape Design Workbook (PDF)](https://www.gardenmyths.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Landscape-Design-workbook.pdf)
- "How many people would you like to entertain outside from time to time?" Seating preferences: do you prefer to sit in sun or shade? — [Melissa Morton](https://melissamorton.co.uk/garden-design-questionnaire/); [Emily Hillier](https://www.emilyhilliergardendesign.com/your-garden-questions)
- What do you see first when arriving home or from key windows? This reveals the priority locations. — [iScapeit, 10 questions before designing a client's yard](https://iscapeit.com/blog/10-questions-to-ask-before-designing-a-clients-yard)
- Outdoor activities, long-term plans for pools, tennis courts or spas, household size and other users. — [Jotform design questionnaire](https://form.jotform.com/212343687298062); [Landscaping Network questionnaire](https://www.landscapingnetwork.com/landscape-design/questionnaire.html)
- How will people move through the space? Complex gardens can be thought of as a series of "rooms", each connecting clearly to the next. — [Fine Gardening](https://www.finegardening.com/article/6-questions-garden-designers-ask-before-they-plan-a-new-garden)

**Style and feel**
- Formal or informal? Which garden styles do you like? Bring photos of gardens you like or browse examples online. — [Landscaping Network](https://www.landscapingnetwork.com/landscape-design/questionnaire.html) (via search summary)
- Choose a unified theme, such as particular colours or a softer, more natural feel. — [UF/IFAS Gardening Solutions](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/design/ten-planning-tips-for-design/)
- How full do you want the garden to look? — [Perrico Gardens](https://perricogardens.com/gardenjournal/questions-for-garden-planning)

**Likes, dislikes and problems**
- What do you like and dislike about the current site, and what do you want? — [Swanson's Nursery](https://swansonsnursery.com/blog/garden-design-101)
- What do you dislike about the current garden? — [Anna McArthur](https://www.annamcarthur.co.uk/garden-questions/); [The Southern Landscape Group questionnaire](https://www.tslg.org/blog/tslg-landscape-design-questionnaire/)
- Survey questions "help focus priorities and hone in on likes or dislikes, all before digging a single hole". — [Fine Gardening](https://www.finegardening.com/article/6-questions-garden-designers-ask-before-they-plan-a-new-garden)

**Plants loved, hated, allergies and safety**
- Hay fever, asthma or plant allergies. If children play outside, avoiding thorny plants may be wise. — [Low Maintenance Landscape, 2022 questionnaire (PDF)](https://lowmaintenancelandscape.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/LML-Design-questionare-2022.pdf); [Melissa Morton](https://melissamorton.co.uk/garden-design-questionnaire/)
- Plant dislikes were the least commonly covered topic across the forms found. — search synthesis of the questionnaires above

**Maintenance**
- "How much time do you hope to spend on landscape maintenance weekly?" Some forms use a 1 to 10 maintenance-level rating. — [Southern Landscape Group](https://www.tslg.org/blog/tslg-landscape-design-questionnaire/); [Low Maintenance Landscape](https://lowmaintenancelandscape.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/LML-Design-questionare-2022.pdf)
- "Who will look after the garden day-to-day, yourself or with the help of a gardener?" — [Emily Hillier](https://www.emilyhilliergardendesign.com/your-garden-questions); [Melissa Morton](https://melissamorton.co.uk/garden-design-questionnaire/)
- Are you willing to keep up the garden so it keeps its intended design? — [Here By Design, nine things to ask](https://herebydesign.net/nine-things-to-ask-yourself-before-designing-a-garden/)
- Lawns cost less up front but more in long-term maintenance. — [Garden Myths workbook](https://www.gardenmyths.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Landscape-Design-workbook.pdf) (via search summary)

**Budget and timeframe**
- Discuss budget, timeline, process and the designer's fee at the first meeting. — [iScapeit](https://iscapeit.com/blog/10-questions-to-ask-before-designing-a-clients-yard)
- Rough or ballpark budget, and whether the work can be phased. — [My Garden Design](https://mygardendesign.co.uk/building-a-garden-design-brief); [Grassroots Garden Design consultation checklist 2026 (PDF)](https://grassrootsgardendesign.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Garden-consultatin-checklist-2026.pdf)
- Todd Haiman's list ends with: is the budget realistic, and can the construction be phased? — [Todd Haiman, 10 questions before designing a garden](https://www.toddhaimanlandscapedesign.com/gardening-resources/10-questions-you-should-ask-before-designing-a-garden)
- How long until the garden should look mature? — [Swanson's Nursery](https://swansonsnursery.com/blog/garden-design-101)

**Accessibility and ageing in place**
- Ask about disabilities, mobility needs, and whether the garden should suit a pushchair or wheelchair. Designers need to know about mobility issues "now or in the future". — [My Garden Design](https://mygardendesign.co.uk/building-a-garden-design-brief); [Melissa Morton](https://melissamorton.co.uk/garden-design-questionnaire/)

**Sustainability, wildlife and water**
- APLD's sustainability checklist starts with discussing client needs and desires, plus soil, watershed and drainage evaluation. — [APLD sustainability checklist](https://apld.memberclicks.net/assets/Sustainability/Design%20Sustainability.pdf)
- Mediterranean and dry-climate briefs add: local water rules and irrigation restrictions, keeping lawn irrigation off dry-planting beds, soil drainage and slope, fire-hazard zone and defensible-space rules, winter wet and humidity tolerance. — [Gardenia.net](https://www.gardenia.net/guide/mediterranean-gardens-designs-plant-lists-care) (commercial guide)

### Inferences: consolidated interview script for the AI assistant
(Synthesised from the sources above. Ordered so it flows as a conversation. Questions marked (P) are partly answerable from photos and should be confirmed, not asked cold.)

**A. Location and site facts (ask first; they drive plant choice)**
1. Where is the garden (town or postcode)? Coastal, urban or rural? Roughly what altitude? (gives climate and hardiness zone)
2. Which direction does the back of the house face? If you stand at the back door looking out, where does the sun set?
3. Which parts get sun in the morning, at midday and in the evening? Does this change much in winter? (P)
4. What's the soil like: sticky clay, sandy and free-draining, chalky or stony? Do rhododendrons, camellias or azaleas do well locally? Ever tested the pH?
5. Does water sit anywhere after rain? Any boggy or very dry spots? (P)
6. Is it windy or exposed? Which direction does the worst wind come from? Any frost pockets?
7. Any known underground pipes, cables, drains, septic tank or soakaway? Any rules to follow (tree protection orders, conservation area, HOA, fire zone, hosepipe or water restrictions)?
8. Rough dimensions? (P: estimate from photos, confirm one known measurement)

**B. People and use**
9. Who uses the garden: you, a partner, grandchildren (ages), visitors, pets?
10. What do you want to do out there? Sit and relax, eat outdoors, entertain (how many people?), garden as a hobby, grow food or herbs, play space for grandchildren, drying washing, storage, compost, a workshop or shed?
11. Where do you like to sit, and at what time of day? Sun or shade?
12. What do you see from the windows you use most (kitchen, living room)? What should you see?
13. How do you move through the garden: routes to the bins, shed, gate, washing line?

**C. Feel and style**
14. Formal and tidy, or relaxed and natural? Which words describe how you want it to feel: calm, colourful, lush, cottagey, modern, Mediterranean, wildlife-rich?
15. Gardens or pictures you love (share photos, Pinterest or Instagram)? Favourite colours, or colours you dislike?
16. How full do you want it: lots of open space, or packed with planting?
17. How should it relate to the house (materials, colours, style)?

**D. Likes, dislikes and must-keeps**
18. What do you love about the garden now, and what must stay (plants, trees, features, sentimental items)? (P: confirm the candidates seen in photos)
19. What bothers you about it now: problems, eyesores, overlooking, maintenance chores you hate?
20. Plants you love? Plants you hate? Any allergies (hay fever, asthma), skin irritants, or thorny or poisonous plants to avoid (grandchildren, pets)?
21. Views to hide, and views to frame or borrow? (P)

**E. Maintenance and ability**
22. Realistically, how many hours a week, and in which seasons, will you spend on the garden? Rate the maintenance you want from 1 (minimal) to 10 (happy pottering daily).
23. Who does the work: you, family, a paid gardener? What do you enjoy doing (deadheading, mowing, veg) and what do you avoid?
24. How experienced are you as a gardener?
25. Do you want a lawn? (Low up-front cost, but ongoing mowing.)

**F. Accessibility and the future**
26. Any mobility issues now, or likely in the next 10 to 20 years? Are steps, uneven paths or kneeling a problem? Would raised beds, handrails, wider level paths or seating along routes help?
27. Lighting needs for evening use or safety?
28. How long do you plan to stay? How soon do you want it to look mature?

**G. Wildlife, sustainability, water**
29. Is attracting birds, bees, butterflies or hedgehogs important? Is a pond wanted, or ruled out for safety?
30. Water use: rainwater harvesting, drought-tolerant planting, irrigation? Any watering restrictions? (Essential in Mediterranean and western US climates.)
31. Peat-free, organic, reuse of existing materials, local stone?

**H. Budget and timing**
32. Rough budget band for build and plants (not design)? Is this all at once, or spread over a few years (phasing)?
33. DIY, contractor or a mix?
34. Any deadline (event, season)? When should work start?

**Close:** Summarise the brief back in about one page and confirm it before designing (mirrors the professional written brief).

## 4. Turning the brief into a concept (mood boards, bubble diagrams, zoning)

### Takeaway
Designers move from brief to layout via (1) zoning: broad public, private and service territories, positioned relative to the house, light, views and access; (2) bubble diagrams: loose bubbles for each function (patio, lawn, play, veg, shed) and their relationships; (3) mood boards for atmosphere, materials and planting palette; then (4) a concept plan that resolves bubbles into shapes, paths and levels.

### Cited Findings
- Bubble diagrams turn a program into a visual of functional relationships. Zoning diagrams set the position and orientation of functions relative to the site and each other, "considering natural light, views, and access." — [Scribd, Bubble and zoning diagrams in design](https://es.scribd.com/document/699622549/Bubble-and-zoning-diagram)
- Order: zoning first (broad public, private and service territories), then bubbles within each zone. — [Learn Architecture, zoning diagram guide](https://learnarchitecture.net/architectural-diagrams/33891-zoning-diagram-guide.html)
- A garden bubble drawing is "a loose sketch that maps out the different areas of your yard. Each bubble represents a functional space: patio, play area, lawn, vegetable garden, shed, etc." Rule of thumb: about two-thirds open functional areas (patios, lawns, walkways) to one-third planting. — [Pretty Purple Door, bubble drawings for landscape design](https://www.prettypurpledoor.com/landscape-bubble-drawing/)
- "The single most important consideration at this stage... is the relationship of the various drawn spaces to the house and to the other functional areas." — [Garden Design Exposed, drawing the garden plan](https://www.gardendesignexposed.com/garden_plan.html)
- Garden-specific bubble diagram guidance. — [Paper Garden Workshop, bubble diagrams](https://www.papergardenworkshop.com/blog/designing-your-garden-bubble-diagrams)
- Concepts combine plan-based concept drawings (zones and paths) with mood boards for atmosphere and materials. — [iScapeit](https://www.iscapeit.com/blog/how-landscape-pros-can-help-clients-understand-design-ideas-faster)
- Concept stage at UK firms: concept plan, mood board, 3D impression, sample hard-landscape materials. — [South Hams Garden Design, Outline Design](https://www.southhamsgardendesign.co.uk/outline-design); [Ivy Hill Garden Design](https://www.ivyhillgardendesign.co.uk/create-your-garden)
- Sketch design uses early sketches, mood boards or digital models showing layout, plants and materials for client feedback. — [Sitescape Design](https://sitescapedesign.com/about-us)
- Treat multifaceted gardens as a series of "rooms", each connecting to the next. — [Fine Gardening](https://www.finegardening.com/article/6-questions-garden-designers-ask-before-they-plan-a-new-garden)

### Inferences
- AI method: from the brief, list the functions with size and sun needs (e.g. dining for 6 needs about 3.5 x 3.5 m and evening sun). Place them on the site's sun and view map (seating where the client said they like to sit, veg in the sunniest spot, utility near the side access, screening where photos show overlooking). Then produce two or three alternative bubble arrangements before drawing shapes. Image generation should come after the zoning is agreed, so renders illustrate a coherent layout and not a random makeover.
- A text mood board (style words, 3 to 5 hard materials, colour palette, 8 to 12 signature plants) is a cheap, checkable substitute for a visual board, and can be used as the prompt basis for image models.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source (SGD, textbook) on mood-board composition standards was found. Coverage comes from studios and drawing guides.

## 5. Presenting options and phasing for limited budgets

### Takeaway
Designers present two or three concept options with plain explanations of what each solves. They take feedback on big decisions first and treat the concept as a discussion document. For tight budgets they always complete a master plan first, then phase: grading, drainage and structure first, the most-used area (often the patio off the house) next, planting last, usually over 2 to 3 years, sequenced so that later phases don't undo earlier ones.

### Cited Findings
- Show two or three clear options, each with a simple explanation of what it solves. Limit early feedback to major decisions to keep revisions structured. — [iScapeit, help clients understand design ideas](https://www.iscapeit.com/blog/how-landscape-pros-can-help-clients-understand-design-ideas-faster)
- A brief agreed in advance guides the concept, estimate, presentation, revisions and final approval. — [iScapeit](https://www.iscapeit.com/blog/how-landscape-pros-can-help-clients-understand-design-ideas-faster)
- The concept is a discussion document open to modification. — [Compass Garden Design](https://www.compassgardendesign.co.uk/the-garden-design-process-explained/)
- Phasing without a master plan is "a common and costly mistake". Example: trees planted before knowing where a future patio will go may have to be dug up. — [Houzz, phased landscape design project](https://www.houzz.com/magazine/what-you-should-know-about-a-phased-landscape-design-project-stsetivw-vs~165314353)
- Grading is easiest and cheapest done all at once, so it belongs in phase 1. — [NALP The Edge, phased landscape projects](https://blog.landscapeprofessionals.org/strategic-spaces-navigating-the-design-build-process-for-phased-landscape-projects/)
- Start with the most-used area, often the patio or deck off the back of the house. If money is tight, build a smaller version of the final footprint with better materials. Prioritise surfaces, hardscape and shade early. — [John Montgomery Landscape Architects, design on a budget](https://jmontgomerydesigns.com/design-on-a-budget/); [Arizona Municipal Water Users Association, finalize your plan](https://www.amwua.org/landscaping-with-style/design/finalize-your-plan)
- Phasing adds mobilisation costs and exposes the client to price rises, so some designers prefer a single-phase install where possible. — [NALP The Edge](https://blog.landscapeprofessionals.org/strategic-spaces-navigating-the-design-build-process-for-phased-landscape-projects/)
- A master plan can typically be phased over 2 to 3 years, highest-impact areas first. — [DZ Insights, landscape design services guide](https://www.dzinsights.com/blog/landscape-design-services)
- Avoid gates, fences or walls that would block installation access for later phases. — [Jefferson Landscape, phased installation](https://www.jeffersonlandscape.com/post/phased-landscape-installation)
- Caveat: most phasing sources are landscape companies with a commercial interest. Cost figures found were local and are not reported here.

### Inferences
- Suggested phasing template for the assistant: Phase 0, clear, remove and keep decisions, plus soil improvement. Phase 1, levels, drainage, services (lighting ducts, irrigation pipes, water butt), main paths and the primary seating or patio. Phase 2, boundaries, screening, structural trees and hedges (they take longest to mature, so plant early where they won't be disturbed). Phase 3, beds and perennial planting. Phase 4, finishing (lighting fixtures, pots, ornaments). Each phase should leave the garden usable and tidy.
- For an older homeowner, phase safety and access items (level paths, handrails, lighting) first, even ahead of aesthetics.

### Gaps
- No SGD or APLD guidance on phasing was retrieved. US sources dominate here, and UK or Mediterranean phasing norms were not specifically found (Mediterranean practice would time planting to autumn rains; see the establishment-watering note in section 2, but no direct phasing source was found).

## 6. What a typical design package includes

### Takeaway
A full package typically includes a measured survey (1:50 or 1:100) and site analysis, a written brief, a concept plan with mood board (often a 3D impression), a scaled and annotated master plan with short specifications and construction notes, a planting plan with plant schedule (names, quantities, sizes, spacing), and optionally lighting plans, construction details, a materials schedule, tender documents, site supervision and a maintenance plan.

### Cited Findings
- Common core: measured survey, concept or layout plan, planting plan with schedule, often a 3D visual. Lighting plans and formal specifications are less consistent. — [Elite Service Solutions](https://www.eliteservicesolutions.co.uk/blog/whats-actually-included-in-garden-design-package/); [Urban Plot, package features](https://www.urbanplot.co.uk/garden-design-packages-included); [Flourish Landscaping, designs and plans](https://flourishlandscaping.co.uk/garden-design-landscaping-services/designs-and-plans/)
- One full package includes a plan of the whole garden plus a 3D or elevation concept visual of part of the garden. — [Tris Williams Garden Design, services](https://www.triswilliamsgardendesign.com/services)
- Planting plan: species, quantities, sizes, spacing, with symbols and a legend of common and botanical names. — [Elite Service Solutions](https://www.eliteservicesolutions.co.uk/blog/whats-actually-included-in-garden-design-package/)
- A medium-sized package may include 3D visuals and a lighting plan. Specifications often appear as a materials schedule and construction notes. — [Garden.com, garden design costs 2026](https://www.garden.com/design/garden-design-costs-2026-what-you-should-expect-to-pay); [HDLG garden design](https://hdlg.co.uk/services/garden-design)
- One 2026 US cost guide lists full packages for small gardens at about $1,500 to $3,500, with planting-only work priced lower. — [Garden.com](https://www.garden.com/design/garden-design-costs-2026-what-you-should-expect-to-pay) (single commercial source; treat as indicative)
- Master plan with short-form specification and construction notes is what contractors need to price the job. — [Sitescape Design](https://sitescapedesign.com/about-us)
- Handover maintenance plan. — [Butler and Parker](https://www.butlerandparker.com/process)
- Many firms sell tiers (e.g. consultation only, planting plan only, full design). — [PC Landscapes, what to expect from a design proposal](https://pclandscapes.co.uk/2026/09/25/what-to-expect-from-a-professional-garden-design-proposal/); [Bestall, services garden designers offer](https://bestall.co/from-the-garden/what-services-do-garden-designers-offer/)

### Inferences
- AI-deliverable equivalent: (1) one-page brief, (2) site analysis summary (annotated photos: sun, views, keep and remove, problems), (3) two or three concept options (zoning description plus image renders), (4) chosen master plan description with an approximate scaled sketch and materials list, (5) planting plan and schedule (botanical and common names, quantity, size at purchase, spacing, position, why chosen, hardiness rating checked against the client's zone), (6) phased budget and implementation order, (7) seasonal maintenance calendar matched to the client's stated hours per week.

### Gaps
- Package pricing for the UK and Mediterranean regions was not found from reliable sources.
- No source detailed the standard scale or format conventions for planting plans beyond the survey scale (1:50 or 1:100).

### Regional differences noted (summary)
- **UK:** SGD work stages, RHS H1 to H7 hardiness, typical north- and south-facing aspect framing, measured survey by designer or surveyor (client often pays), tender to landscape contractors. Sources: above.
- **US:** APLD framework (client interview, site inventory and analysis, base plan, concept, master plan), USDA 2023 zones (plus Sunset zones in the West), property survey, zoning and code and HOA research, design-build firms common, phased installs heavily discussed. Sources: above.
- **Mediterranean-climate (Med basin, California, etc.):** water restrictions, hydrozoning, drainage for dry winter crowns, establishment watering in the first summer, fire-zone defensible space, gravel mulches. Sources are mainly commercial guides; verify with local authorities.
