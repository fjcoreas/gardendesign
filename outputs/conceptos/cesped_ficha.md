# Ficha canónica: zona césped, concepto A "Marco ordenado"

*Aprobado por el usuario el 2026-11-16.* Este párrafo (en inglés) se copia **palabra por palabra** en cada prompt de la zona césped, para que todas las fotos muestren el mismo diseño.

## Resumen en español
- Césped despejado y parejo: es la zona de juego de los nietos.
- Bordillo plano de concreto gris claro, ~15 cm, a ras del suelo.
- Arriate del muro de ladrillo de ~1.2 m: al frente, un seto bajo recortado de limonaria (~55 cm); detrás, rosales en grupos de 3, uno por módulo entre columnas (rojo, blanco y rosado, repetidos); suelo cubierto de maní forrajero de flor amarilla.
- Frente al muro de block: una banca de exhibición de madera tratada a ~80 cm con los bonsáis; arriba, una repisa con las rosas del desierto; helechos y orquídeas colgados de la celosía.
- Carrete de manguera verde en el muro de block. Fuera la estructura oxidada, el banquito y la manguera suelta.

- **Zona de juegos (2026-11-17):** en el extremo del césped junto al aguacate, del lado de la terraza y fuera de la copa: juego compacto de madera (torre de ~1 m, resbaladero verde, arenero con tapa bajo la plataforma, 2 columpios, uno de bebé) sobre tapetes de caucho tipo grama.

## Design spec (canonical, English)
The lawn is an open, even, freshly mown rectangle of tropical grass with nothing standing on it. A flat light-grey concrete mowing strip about 15 cm wide, flush with the grass, edges every planting bed in a clean straight line. Along the red-brick wall the planting bed is about 1.2 m deep: at the front, a low, neatly clipped, continuous hedge of orange jessamine (Murraya paniculata), about 55 cm tall, glossy dark green with a few small white flowers; behind it, rose bushes in groups of three, one group centred in each bay between the concrete pillars, alternating red, white and soft pink and repeating along the wall; the soil between the roses is fully covered by a low groundcover of perennial peanut (Arachis pintoi) with small yellow flowers, with no bare soil visible. In front of the tall grey concrete-block wall stands a long, sturdy display bench of honey-toned treated wood about 80 cm high holding a neat row of bonsai trees in shallow glazed pots, with a single higher wooden shelf above it holding a few desert-rose bonsai; a few Boston ferns and orchids hang in tidy rows from the white lattice. A green wall-mounted hose reel is fixed to the block wall next to the tap. The old rusty metal plant stand, the loose hose and the plastic stool are gone. At the end of the lawn nearest the avocado tree, but out from under its branches, is a compact children's play area laid on a square of green rubber grass mats set flush into the lawn: a small natural-wood playset with a low tower about 1 m high, a sandbox under its platform, a short green plastic slide and a wooden swing beam with two swings, one of them a toddler bucket seat. The middle of the lawn stays open for running and ball games.
