# Wiring a microphone ring to Claude Code

You can build a working "talk into a ring, and Claude works on my branch" prototype now without designing any hardware. Two pieces already exist. The **Pebble Index 01** ($75 preorder, $99 at retail, reported) is a push-to-talk microphone ring with an open-source phone app, and it can forward raw audio or transcripts to any webhook ([Pebble Developers](https://developer.repebble.com/index-01/)). On the Claude side, a Claude Code **cloud scheduled task** (the feature formerly called "routines") can be started with a single authenticated HTTP `POST`, and it then runs against a repository and a branch you choose ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)). The harder part is the rest of your spec: one ring with a microphone, Bluetooth, an IMU and health sensors. No consumer ring found does all of that. Every microphone ring on sale or announced (Pebble, Sandbar Stream, Vocci, Wizpr, Spark, Aina) drops health sensing. The only documented platform with a microphone plus PPG, SpO2, temperature and an IMU is Goertek's **Comma 2 Listener** reference design, which is not a retail product ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/goertek-unveils-next-generation-smart-ring-reference-designs-a-leap-forward-in-health-monitoring-and-interactive-technology-302346537.html)). Some Shenzhen sellers list "recording + health" rings from about $29 a unit at 1,000 units. Battery physics is why the category looks this way. A 15–25 mAh ring cell cannot run a microphone all day, so every product records only while you press, touch, or raise the ring to your mouth. The product idea is also less open than it looks. **Nexting** (MIT-licensed, supports Claude Code, ring in private beta) and **Natura Interface** (press, speak and release to send a request to agents including Claude Code) already target the same use. A note on sources: several researchers could only read search-result snippets, because sites such as TechCrunch, Engadget and Hackster were blocked by network settings. Prices, battery figures and ship dates below are therefore *as reported* by the cited page, not checked against the primary source.

**Consumer products** are the fastest route to a prototype but not to a shipping product. Pebble's design is the best fit for a voice-command interface. It records only while the button is held, stores about 5 minutes of audio on the ring when the phone is out of range, and broadcasts a BLE advertisement on every press and release ([Pebble Developers](https://developer.repebble.com/index-01/)). It also ships with a sealed silver-oxide battery reported at roughly **12–14 hours of total recording over the ring's life** ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/smart-rings/the-new-pebble-smart-ring-wont-track-your-health-doesnt-need-to-be-recharged-and-costs-just-usd75-heres-why-i-cant-wait-to-try-one)). That budget suits short commands and rules out dictating meetings. Pebble reported mass production in July 2026, with new orders shipping in one to two months ([Pebble blog](https://repebble.com/blog/pebble-mega-update-july-2026)). The mainstream health rings (Oura, Samsung, Ultrahuman, RingConn, Circular) appear to have no microphone. That rests on a buying-guide generalization, not a confirmed spec for each model ([Alibaba buying guide](https://electronics.alibaba.com/buyingguides/ring-voice-recorder-guide-which-one-actually-works)).

**OEM/ODM manufacturers** are where the combined mic-plus-health ring would come from, but no factory sells it as a finished, private-label model. Goertek's Comma 2 Listener is described as using reflective and transmissive PPG with "medical-grade blood oxygen monitoring", plus an HD microphone "as the entrance to the AI assistant", body temperature, an IMU, a vibration motor, more than 5 days of battery and IP68 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/goertek-unveils-next-generation-smart-ring-reference-designs-a-leap-forward-in-health-monitoring-and-interactive-technology-302346537.html)). Goertek is a tier-1 ODM and publishes no MOQ or NRE, so expect a startup to need volume or a strong pitch. The more realistic route is a mid-tier Shenzhen ODM. Reported terms: white-labelling an existing platform "typically starts around 500 units", while a new industrial and mechanical design starts around 2,000 ([GMIC.AI](https://gmic.ai/white-label-ai-voice-recorder/)). Custom molds and firmware add **$5,000–20,000** ([Pineeon](https://pineeon.com/smart-ring-oem-vs-odm/)), and a fully new hardware program runs **$150k–500k** in NRE ([Starking](https://starkingsmartring.com/smart-ring-private-label-private-label-brands-guide/)). Those figures come from supplier blogs and should be treated as marketing. In practice there are two ways to get your spec. One is to ask a health-ring ODM such as J-Style to add a microphone. The other is to ask a mic-ring ODM to add PPG and temperature. Both count as custom ODM work, not private label. Whichever you choose, the contract should cover five things: a documented BLE audio service, the codec, a record-then-sync mode, your right to ship your own firmware, and a plan to re-certify if firmware changes radio behavior. Some ODMs restrict firmware changes because they can void FCC/CE certification ([Alibaba seller blog](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/consumer-electronics/smart-watch-ce-fcc-rohs-certification-guide-alibaba-b2b)).

**Dev kits and components** are the path to your own ring. Nordic's **nRF54L15** is now the default BLE chip in new rings. It comes in a 2.45 × 2.25 mm package and draws about 3.4 mA receiving and 4.8 mA transmitting ([Nordic](https://www.nordicsemi.com/Products/nRF54L15)). It is already sold as ring-ready modules: the Bravechip BCL603S2H chiplet and the Hongjia HJ-N54L SiP ([Nordic news](https://www.nordicsemi.com/Nordic-news/2026/01/Tangshan-Hongjias-employs-Nordics-nRF54L15-SoC-to-power-smart-rings-and-hearing-aids)). Ambiq and Bravechip's Apollo330B Plus SiP lists voice wake-up and transcription among its features ([Ambiq](https://ambiq.com/news/ambiq-and-bravechip-cut-smart-ring-costs-by-85-with-new-edge-ai-chiplet/)). One caveat: Nordic staff say the nRF54L lacks an audio PLL and has only "limited" LE Audio support ([DevZone](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/117778/nrf54l15-support-le-audio)). For real-time LC3 streaming, the nRF5340 used in Omi's open hardware is the safer choice ([Omi docs](https://docs.omi.me/doc/hardware/consumer)). The sensor shortlist:

- **TDK T5838 microphone:** about 20 µA in acoustic-activity-detect mode and about 310 µA when recording at high quality ([TDK](https://invensense.tdk.com/products/digital/t5838/)).
- **ST LSM6DSV16X IMU:** about 19 µA while still, and its on-chip machine-learning core can classify motion without waking the main chip ([ST community](https://community.st.com/t5/mems-and-sensors/how-to-save-power-with-lsm6dsv16x-by-leveraging-on-its-adaptive/ta-p/580690)).
- **ADI MAX86176 PPG + ECG front-end:** 2.7 mm square, under 11 µA per optical channel ([ADI](https://www.analog.com/en/products/max86176.html)).

About measuring speed: a ring IMU gives acceleration and rotation, which is enough for steps, cadence, activity type and gestures. In engineering terms, true speed from integrating acceleration drifts quickly. Expect speed to come from fusing the ring's cadence with the phone's GPS, not from the ring alone.

## A 20 mAh battery forces push-to-talk

Battery size sets the interaction design. Samsung's Galaxy Ring carries **18–23.5 mAh** depending on size ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Galaxy_Ring)). Curved ring cells are listed at 16–24 mAh, and one seller quotes $1.55–1.85 each at 1,000 units ([Ufine](https://www.ufinebattery.com/products/3-7-v-20mah-curved-battery-150732), [Global Sources](https://www.globalsources.com/knowledge/lithium-polymer-battery-for-smart-rings/)). Combining the component figures above gives a rough budget. A microphone recording at about 0.3 mA, plus processing and codec, plus BLE radio comes to about **2–5 mA on average**. With 16 mAh usable, that means continuous streaming would last only **about 3–8 hours**. This is the researchers' own calculation, not a measurement. Running a full-power microphone with keyword spotting all day would use more than an entire ring battery. Hardware listening for sound at about 20 µA costs only about 0.5 mAh per day, but in a noisy setting it keeps waking the chip. The products that ship reflect this: Pebble records while held, Sandbar on touch, and Wizpr when the ring is raised to the mouth. Their claims cluster around **10–16 hours of active recording**. Wizpr is reported at 10 hours by VTouch and 14 hours by TechRadar ([VTouch](https://www.vtouch.io/en-us/products/wizpr-ring), [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/fitness-trackers/this-smart-ring-is-actually-a-secret-microphone-used-to-interface-with-ai-chatbots)). Sandbar claims "16+ hours of frequent use" ([Sandbar](https://www.sandbar.com/stream?pubDate=20251106)). For a command interface (say twenty 30-second requests a day), push-to-talk with store-and-forward gives days to weeks per charge. Adding PPG and temperature sampling eats into that budget, which helps explain why no shipping ring combines them.

Bandwidth is not the constraint. Speech needs only 16 kHz sampling. The LE Audio voice preset carries it at **32 kbps with LC3**, and Bluetooth SIG listening tests found LC3 at 32 kbps matched SBC at 66 kbps ([Bluetooth SIG](https://www.bluetooth.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/LC3Characterization_WP.pdf)). That is a few percent of a 1–2 Mbps BLE link, so ordinary GATT notifications can carry a voice upload without LE Audio. Five minutes of audio at 32 kbps is about 1.2 MB, so buffering on the ring is cheap. Live streaming over GATT can lag: one developer reported **about 1 second of latency** streaming microphone audio on an nRF5340 ([Nordic DevZone](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/129091/nrf5340-live-i2s-microphone-audio-streaming-over-ble-with-low-latency)). For commands that start a coding agent, that lag is irrelevant: record, sync, then transcribe.

The real limits on audio quality are where the microphone sits and how loudly you speak. A finger-worn microphone works as a close-talk mic only when the hand is raised to the mouth, which is why Pebble calls its mic "short range" and Wizpr gates on proximity. Whispering is the trap that marketing hides. Off-the-shelf Whisper scored about **18.8% word error rate on whispered speech**, and fine-tuning brought that to 9.2% ([arXiv 2407.21211](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21211v1)). On normal speech, on-device engines now do very well: Apple's SpeechAnalyzer measured **1.98% WER** with about 130 ms post-speech latency in one Mac benchmark ([DEV](https://dev.to/iravoice/apple-speechanalyzer-vs-whispercpp-a-40-speaker-mac-benchmark-40i4)). Apple's engine supports about 10 languages, against about 100 for WhisperKit ([Argmax](https://www.argmaxinc.com/blog/apple-and-argmax)). That matters if you mix Spanish and English with code identifiers. Cloud transcription costs about **$0.0025–0.008 per minute** ([AssemblyAI](https://assemblyai.com/blog/speech-to-text-api-pricing), [PerkStack](https://perkstack.co/blog/cheapest-gpt-4o-transcribe-api)), which is pennies a day for command use. So users should be told to speak softly rather than truly whisper. A sensible pipeline transcribes on the phone first and optionally re-transcribes in the cloud before triggering code changes.

## Claude cannot hear audio, so a phone app does the hand-off

The Claude Messages API takes text and images, not audio, so speech-to-text must happen first ([Claude API](https://platform.claude.com/docs/api/messages/create)). After that, four documented routes can turn a transcript into work on a GitHub branch. They differ mainly in which credentials they accept and whether a third-party product may use them.

| Route | How the ring app triggers it | Branch control | Auth | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cloud scheduled task (API trigger) | `POST /v1/claude_code/routines/<id>/fire` with `{"text": transcript}` | Saved prompt names the branch; default pushes to `claude/*` | Per-task bearer token; claude.ai Pro/Max/Ultra/Team/Enterprise; research preview | Best for a personal prototype |
| Managed Agents Sessions API | `POST /v1/sessions` with a `github_repository` resource | Explicit `checkout: {type:"branch", name}` | Console API key | Best for a product sold to others |
| Claude Code GitHub Action | Phone creates an issue or comment `@claude <transcript>` via GitHub API | Workflow and prompt decide | API key or `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN`; commenter needs write access | No Anthropic beta; runs on GitHub runners |
| Follow-up to a cloud session | `claude -p "..." --cloud <session-id>` from a server | Inherits the session | claude.ai CLI login only | Multi-turn voice conversation |

Sources: scheduled tasks ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)); Managed Agents ([Claude Platform API](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/http/beta/sessions/create)); GitHub Action ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/github-actions)); follow-ups to a cloud session ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-code-on-the-web)).

The **scheduled-task API trigger** is the most direct "ring button to agent on a repo" hook. The call returns a `claude_code_session_url` that opens in the Claude mobile app's Code tab, so the user can watch or steer the run. Its design suits voice input. The fire text arrives wrapped in a `<routine-fire-payload>` block marked as untrusted data, and Claude acts on it only if the saved prompt explicitly says to. Each token can fire only its own task, is shown once, and can be revoked or rotated. It has limits too. It is a **research preview** behind the beta header `experimental-cc-routine-2026-04-01`. API fires are capped at **30 per hour per task and 100 per hour per account**. Commits appear under your own GitHub identity. Runs start from the default branch and push to `claude/`-prefixed branches unless the prompt says otherwise. The docs also warn that a branch-protection rule your connected access can bypass will not block a push ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/routines)). The **Managed Agents** route gives exact branch checkout and is billed per token plus **$0.08 per running session-hour**. Anthropic's worked example puts a one-hour Opus 5.5 coding session at $0.58 ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Using Sonnet 5.5 at $2/$10 per million input/output tokens, the pipeline researcher estimated about **$0.32 per coding task**. For 20 tasks a day, that is roughly $130–400 a month on the API, depending on model. On a subscription, the scheduled-task route has no marginal cost until you hit plan limits.

The terms of use decide which route a *product* may use. Anthropic's legal page says subscription OAuth "is intended exclusively for purchasers" of its plans for ordinary use of native Anthropic apps. It adds that developers building products "should use API key authentication through Claude Console or a supported cloud provider". It also says Anthropic does not permit third parties "to offer Claude.ai login into their own applications, or to route requests through Free, Pro, Max, or Ultra plan credentials on behalf of their users", or to "collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens" ([Claude Code legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). A personal setup is fine: your own token for your own task, called from your own app. A ring sold to other people should send each user's request through **their own Console API key** on Managed Agents or the Agent SDK. Whether a third-party app may store a user's own scheduled-task token is a grey area the docs do not settle, since the `/fire` endpoint is "available to claude.ai users only". Ask Anthropic before you ship that design. VentureBeat reported a mid-2026 "Agent SDK credit" that would let third-party apps draw on subscriptions ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/anthropic-reinstates-openclaw-and-third-party-agent-usage-on-claude-subscriptions-with-a-catch/)). The current legal page still contains the prohibition, so treat that report as unconfirmed. Remote Control, which steers a local Claude Code session from the phone, explicitly rejects API keys and has no programmatic API. It works as a viewing and approval screen, not an integration point ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control)).

## Your own voice is legal to record; your colleagues' is not

Recording your own spoken commands carries little legal risk almost everywhere. The exposure starts when the ring captures other people. In the US, most states allow recording with one party's consent, but **about 11–13 states, including California and Florida, require every party's consent** for confidential conversations ([Interviewer Signal](https://interviewersignal.com/blog/two-party-consent-states), [Brass Transcripts](https://brasstranscripts.com/blog/state-by-state-recording-laws-2026-guide)). In the EU, regulators read GDPR's household exemption narrowly after the *Ryneš* ruling ([Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/private-video-recordings-made-using-wearables-in-public-spaces)). The EDPB says device makers and AI providers are **controllers in their own right** and cannot push GDPR duties onto wearers ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/edpb-says-smart-glasses-makers-cant-rely-on-wearers-to-meet-gdpr-duties/)). Hamburg's regulator applied the same reasoning against Ray-Ban Meta glasses ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/hamburg-regulator-finds-ray-ban-meta-glasses-expose-bystanders-without-consent/)). So a "record the meeting" mode turns the company into a data controller for bystanders' voices.

In **El Salvador**, Constitution Art. 24 bars intercepting communications without a judge's order ([Asamblea Legislativa](https://www.asamblea.gob.sv/sites/default/files/documents/decretos/02E04307-2B83-4625-B7C6-42D07960F426.pdf)). However, the Constitutional Chamber has held that a participant accepts the other party may record the conversation ([Jurisprudencia.gob.sv](https://www.jurisprudencia.gob.sv/DocumentosBoveda/D/1/2010-2019/2011/01/8CF3D.PDF)). Mexico follows similar participant logic, with criminal penalties for spying or publishing recordings ([RecordingLaw](https://www.recordinglaw.com/es/world-laws/world-recording-laws/mexico-recording-laws/)). On the platform side, Android 14+ blocks an app from starting a microphone foreground service while it is in the background ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)). Ring audio arrives over BLE rather than through the phone's own microphone, but whether a companion app may process it in the background, and the iOS equivalent, still needs confirming. Health sensing brings its own regulation: a ring marketed for clinical SpO2 enters medical-device territory, as shown by Movano's FDA 510(k) clearance ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/movano-health-secures-fda-clearance-eviemed-ring)). The design choice that settles most of this is the one the battery already forces: hold-to-talk capture of the wearer's own voice, transcribed on the device, with audio discarded after transcription and no ambient or meeting mode in version one.

There is also a security reason to avoid always-on listening. Ambient speech reaching a code-executing agent is a prompt-injection channel: anyone near you could "instruct" your agent. Push-to-talk, a confirmation tap before firing, and draft-only PRs against a protected `main` keep merging a human decision.

## Two startups already ship ring-to-Claude-Code

The idea sounds unclaimed, but it is not. One researcher concluded that no ring yet turns speech into a developer-agent action. The pipeline researcher found two that do. **Nexting** describes itself as a "wearable agent dispatcher" for your own agents, including Claude Code, Codex and OpenClaw. It is MIT-licensed, its PIN sells for **$129**, and its ring is in private beta ([GitHub wiki](https://github.com/Nexting-ai/nexting/wiki), [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/nexting-2)). **Natura Interface** is a ring where you press, speak and release to send a request to your personal agent, with Claude Code among the listed agents ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/natura-launches-interface-a-smart-ring-for-voice-requests-that-connect-to-your-personal-ai-agents/)). Neither appears to combine voice with health sensing, and neither has public developer documentation beyond Nexting's repository.

Others sit nearby:

- **Vocci** has an MCP server that lets Claude and Claude Code query what the ring recorded. It is read-only and pull-based: Claude consults it only when you name Vocci, and the ring cannot push a command ([Vocci](https://vocci.ai/pages/connect-your-ai), [note.com review](https://note.com/uemin1959/n/n004db8b24445?hl=en)).
- **Pebble** builds each automatic action as an MCP tool and sends raw audio or transcripts to webhooks ([Pebble Developers](https://developer.repebble.com/index-01/)).
- **Sandbar**, with about $36M raised, brands itself "a wearable conversational interface for an agentic world" but has no public API ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/sandbar-secures-23m-series-a-for-its-ai-note-taking-ring/)).
- **Aina** ($5.5M seed) is the only ring with a screen and speaker that could display "PR opened" or "tests failed". It targets consumer agents such as Uber and ships in January 2027, iPhone and Mac only ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/05/ainas-smart-ai-ring-promises-a-new-way-to-interact-with-ai-agents-that-anticipate-users-needs/)).
- **Omi** is a pendant, not a ring, but it sets the standard for openness: MIT hardware and firmware, real-time transcript webhooks, and 250+ apps ([Omi docs](https://docs.omi.me/)).
- **Hobby projects** already pipe voice into Claude Code from an Apple Watch (Handwave) and from AR glasses (claude-code-g2) ([zackproser.com](https://zackproser.com/blog/handwave), [GitHub](https://github.com/sam-siavoshian/claude-code-g2)).

Big tech has bought always-listening wearables (Amazon bought Bee, Meta bought Limitless) rather than ring makers, and Limitless stopped selling hardware afterwards ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/12/05/meta-acquires-ai-device-startup-limitless/)). That leaves several openings. A combined health-and-voice ring is one. Branch-scoped safety (dry run, confirm, draft PR) is another, since no competitor addresses it. A "bring your own Claude" model that avoids the $10–30 per month subscriptions Sandbar and Vocci charge is a third ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/wearables/voccis-ai-note-taking-ring-aims-to-do-much-more-170536442.html)).

## The fastest prototype needs no hardware at all

**Stage 1 (days, no ring):** prove the agent loop with the phone alone.

1. Create one cloud scheduled task per target repo and branch. Its saved prompt should say: act only on the request in the `routine-fire-payload` block; work on branch X; open a draft PR; never touch `main`, CI workflows or secrets.
2. Store the task's token in the iOS Keychain or Android Keystore.
3. Build a small app or iOS Shortcut that records, transcribes on the device, shows the transcript for a confirming tap, and POSTs it to `/fire`. The Claude iOS app already exposes Siri and Shortcuts intents, but those reach chat, not a repo-aware agent ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10263469-using-claude-app-intents-shortcuts-and-widgets-on-ios)).
4. Turn on GitHub branch protection that your connected identity cannot bypass.

If the beta endpoint feels too fragile, use a fine-grained single-repo GitHub token to post an `@claude` issue instead, and let the GitHub Action do the work.

**Stage 2 (weeks, off-the-shelf rings):** add the ring.

1. Buy a Pebble Index 01 and point its webhook at a small receiver that calls `/fire`. Alternatively, fork its open-source phone app and add the HTTP action there.
2. Return status through the phone notification, Pebble's RGB LED, or earbuds.
3. For the motion and health data, add a Colmi R02. Its open Python client and modifiable firmware expose accelerometer and heart-rate data cheaply ([tahnok/colmi_r02_client](https://github.com/tahnok/colmi_r02_client)). This means two rings for now, which is acceptable for validating whether the sensor data adds value (for example, "don't fire agent runs while I'm driving or running").
4. In parallel, bench-test your own audio firmware on a Seeed XIAO nRF52840 Sense, which has a PDM microphone and an IMU on a thumb-sized board.

**Stage 3 (months, own hardware):** choose between building and buying.

- **Build:** an nRF54L15 ring SiP (Bravechip or Hongjia), a T5838 microphone, an LSM6DSV16X IMU, a MAX86176 PPG front-end, and a 20 mAh curved cell. Use hold-to-talk with on-ring flash buffering and burst sync. The Memfault smart-ring series is a useful engineering guide ([Memfault](https://interrupt.memfault.com/blog/smart-ring-development-part-1)).
- **Buy:** approach a Shenzhen ODM with your required sensor list and the contract terms above. Request Goertek Comma 2 Listener terms if volumes justify it.

Either way, plan for FCC Part 15 and CE certification and UN38.3 testing of the exact cell. The low transmit power should qualify the ring for exclusion from SAR testing under FCC KDB 447498, but an accredited lab must confirm it ([FCC KDB 447498](https://apps.fcc.gov/kdb/GetAttachment.html?id=f8IQgJxTTL5y0oRi0cpAuA%3D%3D&desc=447498+D01+General+RF+Exposure+Guidance+v06&tracking_number=20676)). If you plan to sell the product, switch it to per-user Console API keys on Managed Agents before launch.

## Conclusion

The ring is not where this product's value lies. Voice capture on a finger is solved and getting cheaper. The open questions are trust and safety: making a spoken sentence safe to turn into a commit. That points to a software-first strategy. Build a ring-agnostic bridge (any push-to-talk wearable → transcript → confirmation → branch-scoped Claude run → draft PR → status back to the wrist) and treat hardware as interchangeable until usage shows that combining health sensors with voice matters. The sensor data's most valuable job may be deciding *when not to act* (driving, exercising, asleep), not tracking fitness.

Three dependencies could reshape the plan, so watch them. First, Anthropic's scheduled-task trigger is a research preview whose endpoint, limits and third-party rules may change, so a product built on it should be ready to fall back to Managed Agents or the GitHub Action. Second, Nexting and Natura are already in this exact niche, so differentiation has to come from safety design, health context, or openness rather than from the basic idea. Third, every price, battery figure and ship date above comes from reports, many read only as snippets. Get written quotes and test real units before committing money.
